# 1 Bit Summit: independent review, batch 3

**Scope:** forensic evidence from released Bonsai weights; reasoning recovery using student-generated text and discrepancy rounding; quantization-aware optimization and controlled sign changes. **Research cutoff: 24 November 2026.** This is an independent paper-and-code review, not a trained-model result. No model was trained or evaluated for this review.

## 1. Framing and result threshold

Two qualifications matter before the evidence. First, a **fingerprint of final quantized weights cannot uniquely reveal the training algorithm**. Sign changes, group-scale inflation and weight drift can result from several optimization procedures. Second, a higher score on a different benchmark suite, bit width, model size or corpus is **not** a five-point gain on 1 Bit Summit's target. These are interpretive cautions, not objections to pursuing either line.

The project requires **one sign per weight plus one FP16 scale per 128 weights**, with the tied embedding and output matrix included. The externally reported reference is Qwen3-1.7B FP16 **58.24** and Bonsai 1-bit **40.88** on the same ten-benchmark average. Retention is `40.88 / 58.24 = 70.19%`. A five-percentage-point retention increment requires `0.05 × 58.24 = 2.912` absolute points in the ten-benchmark mean, or **29.12 summed benchmark-score points**, with unchanged evaluation settings. Reference: [PrismML Bonsai paper repository](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo), `1-bit-bonsai-8b-whitepaper.pdf`, table 9. An improvement on only MATH-500, for example, needs +29.12 raw task points if the other nine scores do not change. Gains that break other tasks have to be netted out.

Probability judgments below mean: **my probability that this line, implemented competently and judged against a matched strong binary baseline, delivers at least +5 absolute retention percentage points on the fixed Qwen3-1.7B ten-benchmark suite under the strict storage format**. These are subjective and conditional on getting a working own-model baseline, which is not yet measured. They are not empirical frequencies. "Before" reflects general plausibility before detailed reading; "after" incorporates the specific source evidence. Probabilities for different lines are correlated and must not be added.

### 2B. The exact ThakiCloud measurement, not its headline paraphrase

The [fingerprint implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/scripts/bonsai_repro.py) uses Hugging Face `AutoModelForCausalLM` to load **`Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B`** and **`prism-ml/Bonsai-1.7B-unpacked`** as float16 models. It obtains `named_parameters()` from each, matches parameters by name, and includes only **equal-shaped 2D tensors** while excluding any name containing `embed` or `norm`. Thus its 196 reported objects are compatible with **28 transformer blocks × 7 named projection matrices**, but **not a whole-model audit**. In particular the tied embedding/output interface is omitted. The code uses flattened contiguous groups of 128, not an explicit audit of group orientation in every tensor.

For a matched base matrix `wb` and decoded Bonsai matrix `wq`, it defines:

1. **Sign agreement** = mean over entries of `sign(wq) == sign(wb)`. Per-matrix results are aggregated with **matrix element counts as weights**. Reported **71.62% agreement**, thus **28.38% sign mismatch**. An exact zero in the base has sign zero and therefore counts as mismatch to any nonzero decoded Bonsai entry. Sign mismatch proves different deployed signs, not why they changed.
2. **"Identical to naive BWN"** = fraction of entries satisfying `torch.isclose(wq, quant_binary(wb), rtol=0.01, atol=0.0001)`. The naive binary weight is `mean(abs(wb)) × sign(wb)` for each flattened group of 128, with zeros assigned positive. Its reported **0.03%** is an element-weighted tolerance match, **not** the fraction of weights that underwent training. Inflated scales alone can drive this number nearly to zero despite preserved signs.
3. **Group scale estimate** = `amax(abs(wq_group))` over each group of 128. The naive denominator = `mean(abs(wb_group))`, clamped to at least `1e-9`. The script first takes the **median of these ratios within each matrix**, then reports the **median of the 196 per-matrix medians**, **2.257×**. This is not the median of all groups pooled together and is not a learned-scale parameter read from a GGUF metadata field. It is inferred from decoded FP16 matrix values. The aggregation treats matrices equally at the final median, irrespective of their sizes.
4. Its record emits a per-matrix `per_layer` array but displays only the first and last four such records in the returned summary. It does **not** calculate sign changes by original weight magnitude decile, by neuron position, by matrix family or by token-frequency band. Those breakdowns cannot be attributed to its published fingerprint.
5. Its **structural check** uses the **first matching Linear module** selected by substring, groups its flattened data, and inspects the **first 2,000 groups**. It rounds absolute values to five decimal places before checking the number of nonzero magnitudes per group. The reported 100% groups with one magnitude and zero zeros apply to that **sampled tensor and precision**, not every projection and every embedding row. The same function's theoretical `1.125 bpw` accounting does not establish a byte-for-byte packed GGUF audit.

Source and definitions: [`fingerprint`, lines 176 onward](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/scripts/bonsai_repro.py); [`verify_bits`, group handling](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/scripts/bonsai_repro.py); [result fields](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/results.json).

ThakiCloud's `quantize_eval` is a different experiment. It quantizes `torch.nn.Linear` parameters, not arbitrary embedding modules, and evaluates a **short fixed repeated text passage**. Its original results list base perplexity **3.507**, Bonsai **3.492**, and a naive rounded model at roughly **2.1 million**. A later frontier table in the same results file gives base **2.027**, Bonsai **1.971**, and an input-rotated public quantizer **4.213**. The two baseline families should **not** be merged into a single controlled comparison without resolving the changed harness, text, checkpoint configuration and decoding conditions. Neither short-passage parity nor a near-identical perplexity proves reasoning, coding, tool calling or the ten-benchmark product score. The salient-weight recovery studies leave 1% or 3% of weights in FP16 and explicitly violate the strict 1.125-bpw format. Sources: [report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/REPORT.md), [results JSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/results.json), [script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/scripts/bonsai_repro.py).

### 2E. How the model was made: what follows and what does not

**Established by external measurements:** first-generation binary body signs differ substantially from base weights; its scales exceed naive mean-absolute baselines; flipped signs concentrate among originally small weights; the tied embedding appears near direct rounding; and the body differs more in weight space than a direct-rounding baseline. These strongly favor **function-aware co-adaptation** over independent per-matrix absmean rounding. Sources: [ThakiCloud script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/scripts/bonsai_repro.py), [Kalaris analysis](https://kalarislabs.com/blog/prismml-bonsai-team-1-bit-reverse-engineering).

**Not established:** an exact loss function, teacher-data recipe, number of processed tokens, existence/absence of STE training, use of mirror descent, original latent weight magnitudes, whether the sign changes occurred by gradient updates or optimized rounding, or the project's legal relationship to any patent. ThakiCloud's assertion that **71.6% sign agreement is consistent with “no retraining”** and Kalaris's assertion that the body **“was retrained”** are mutually different interpretations of similar observables, not experimental determinations of the optimizer. The magnitude-decile profile is compatible with training-induced sign inertia, but a saliency-ranked discrete search can also preferentially modify small-magnitude weights. A precise training-history conclusion requires independent procedural evidence, not just the final checkpoint.

**Cheapest falsification without model training:** obtain two aligned decoded matrices and their exact tokenizer/RoPE metadata; compute (a) per-projection sign flips by magnitude decile, (b) group-scale ratio with three separate aggregation definitions, (c) group-index/row-index/position trends, and (d) tied embedding versus naive absmean. Publish the raw statistics and code. Compare each fingerprint with **known** straight-through recovery, GPTQ-style compensation and discrete-coordinate-search procedures already generated by others. Matching a fingerprint can **reject some mechanisms**, but cannot uniquely certify one.

**Five-point probability, forensic finding alone:** **before 8%, after 11%**. Reason: the forensic evidence offers a more targeted starting hypothesis (body must compensate for a near-rounded tied interface), but it provides no tested Qwen conversion improvement. This is diagnostic value, not a claimed +5.

### 3B. Right On Q: what is trained, against what, on what text

**Problem identified:** A quantized model that begins to generate from the wrong prefix cannot be fully repaired by distillation solely on the teacher's original responses. The student encounters its own previously generated tokens. Teacher-forced KL can look good while its independent long reasoning trajectories repeat, drift or truncate. The paper examines Qwen3 **1.7B, 4B, 8B** at **2-bit and 3-bit weights**, with BF16 activations; **no 1-bit experiment** is shown. Sources: [paper abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29378), [indexed full-paper text](https://bittide.aicompass.dev/article/db0642b6-3533-42ce-9def-2417956be2dd).

**Stage 1: block-wise calibration.** Asymmetric uniform group quantization with size **128** and latent full-precision parameters, scales and zero points. A representative calibration corpus contains **4,096 sequences × 2,048 tokens = 8,388,608 sequence positions** per calibration pass, used in an approximately two-epoch block-wise phase. This is already far more than a tiny 128-window quick calibration and should not be omitted when quoting OnQ's apparent recovery budget. A group of unsigned 2-bit codes with a learned offset is structurally more expressive than one sign plus one FP16 scale. Source: [method transcription](https://daiwk.github.io/auto-research/foundation-models/2610.29378-right-on-q/README-en/), [paper indexed tables](https://bittide.aicompass.dev/article/db0642b6-3533-42ce-9def-2417956be2dd).

**Stage 2: on-policy distillation (OPD).** A **25,600-prompt pool** combines math and coding reasoning with science and general instructions, drawing from OpenMathReasoning, OpenCodeReasoning-2, OpenScienceReasoning-2, Tülu3 and multi-constraint instructions. The quantized student samples **128 continuations per optimizer update**; the frozen full-precision teacher evaluates the token distribution **at those student-generated prefixes**. The 1.7B run uses **400 steps**, i.e. **51,200 sampled completions**, not 51,200 tokens. The other model sizes use 300 steps. The underlying response lengths vary and can extend to several thousand tokens, so **a precise total processed-token budget is not established by a completion count alone**. Constant learning rate approximately `1e-6`, AdamW, and a staged output-length cap are documented. Sources: [indexed original manuscript](https://bittide.aicompass.dev/article/db0642b6-3533-42ce-9def-2417956be2dd), [method reconstruction](https://daiwk.github.io/auto-research/foundation-models/2610.29378-right-on-q/README-en/).

**Objective:** OnQ's candidate-wise **Dynamic KL** weights forward and reverse divergence using the relative teacher/student probability on token `v`. With `p` the frozen teacher, `q` student, and `a_v = stopgrad[p_v/(p_v+q_v)]`, the loss has terms `a_v p_v log(p_v/q_v)` and `(1-a_v) q_v log(q_v/p_v)` summed over candidates. This is neither plain token-level cross-entropy nor task-reward reinforcement learning. Implementationally, the paper describes a union of high-probability teacher/student tokens plus a residual bucket and numerical smoothing. The teacher's logits must be available. Source: [indexed method](https://daiwk.github.io/auto-research/foundation-models/2610.29378-right-on-q/README-en/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29378).

**Why the data mixture matters:** The source's multi-domain training pool is not incidental. On a Qwen3-1.7B W2 comparison, math-only training under the same dynamic KL approach gave approximately **10.98 HumanEval+**, whereas the diverse pool reached about **48.78**. That is an enormous potential **code regression** from a math-specific curriculum. Nevertheless, the on-policy versus fixed-response comparison also changes the prompt set, so not all resulting gain can be assigned uniquely to the dynamic divergence or trajectory choice. Source: [indexed Table 3–4 and appendix descriptions](https://bittide.aicompass.dev/article/db0642b6-3533-42ce-9def-2417956be2dd); [critical evidence map](https://pith.science/paper/2610.29378).

### 3D. DiscQuant: what its result does and does not establish

[DiscQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06417) treats quantization as selecting a rounding among a **fixed predeclared grid**, not training the original network through arbitrary continued pretraining. For each FP weight, choose between adjacent grid points by a continuous relaxation `x_i in [0,1]`; minimize teacher-versus-quantized output divergence on sampled text while regularizing movement to the vertices of the hypercube, then finish rounding. A discrepancy-theoretic argument bounds how many coordinates need remain fractional if empirical gradients lie near a low-dimensional subspace. **Important theorem caveat:** the theory assumes a coordinate-product quantization grid; a learned shared 128-weight scale couples many coordinates, and does not automatically fit the theorem's independent-coordinate setup. Source: [full paper §2–4 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06417); [author code](https://github.com/jerry-chee/DiscQuant).

**Published model evidence:** Phi-3-mini **3.8B** and Llama3.1 **8B**, not 1.7B. The lowest model setting in the main tables is **3.0 bits per weight**, not strict 1-bit. For Phi-3-mini at 3 bits, reported GSM8K was **14.3** for DiscQuant vs **3.6** GPTQ and **0.5** RTN, with **77.0** full-precision teacher on that table's setting; at 3.25 bpw, DiscQuant ~**58.3** vs GPTQ **56.3** and RTN **10.8** (check precise table variants before quoting the paper abstract's 64/54/31, which is another configuration). The paper's abstract cites a 3.25-bit GSM8K comparison **64 / 54 / 31** on a different setting. Both are legitimate as presented by their authors but should not be silently mixed. Sources: [Table 2 and Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06417), [abstract](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v291/chee25a.html).

**Text and cost:** calibration from **RedPajama-1T-Sample**, constructed into up to **2,048-token sequences**, usually **8,192 samples**, and optimized for a tuned number of iterations. Appendix A.1 gives block-scaling study settings including up to **1,024 iterations**, AdamW, learning-rate tuning, projection/clamping, warmup and per-component clipping. At 8,192 × 2,048, one pass represents about **16.78 million token positions**, not necessarily the total backward workload of the algorithm. A math-text mix adds GSM8K/MetaMathQA examples and improves GSM8K, but **PIQA worsens** in the paper's data-mixture study. This is measured task tradeoff, not a universal reasoning-only benefit. Sources: [§5.3, Appendix A.1–A.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06417), [code README](https://github.com/microsoft/DiscQuant).

**Which objective matters:** Its Appendix A.4 found **output-logit KL outperformed intermediate hidden-state L2** in the Phi3 3.25-bit ablation: KL-only GSM8K approximately **64.9**, hidden layer-L2-only **54.1**, linear-L2-only **60.1**, while training on ground-truth tokens got about **52.7**. This is directly relevant to our tendency to optimize local reconstruction, but it does not establish the same ordering at binary precision. Source: [DiscQuant Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06417).

**Applicability:** A discrepancy-guided binary sign assignment is conceptually attractive as an **initializer** from an FP16 original and frozen teacher. But the tested algorithm and proof do not resolve the group-scale coupling, sign flips at a fixed magnitude, tied embeddings or 2B-and-below performance. If the checkpoint is **already binary** and no FP original remains, a pure "round pretrained weights" objective is no longer defined in its original form: it would require an external teacher and a changed discrete-move formulation. Do not advertise DiscQuant's mathematical proof as a proof of 1.125-bpw recovery.

**Five-point probability for a binary adaptation of DiscQuant:** **before 12%, after 9%**. A principled method with large gains over weak rounding baselines at 3 bits, but the actual demonstrated transfer distance is considerable. **Cheapest discriminating proof:** solve a fixed-small-calibration binary sign-selection problem with **shared g128 scales**, compare at equal teacher-forward and backward compute against activation-aware compensation and signed-coordinate descent, then evaluate paired correctness on held-out tasks. Reject if it improves only reconstruction/KL or requires supplemental metadata.

## 4. Item 3: WinQ and controlled code changes during binary training

### 4A. Reading and access record

Read the [WinQ paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17471), including its arXiv HTML-rendered sections, method, Table 13, training details, experiments and appendices; checked the [ICML 2026 published version](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26dr.html). Inspected the [official GitHub repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/WinQ), [periodic reset callback in full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/WinQ/main/utils/reinitialize_callback.py), [demonstration launch script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/WinQ/main/scripts/1_run_train.sh) and the quantized-linear implementation cited by the repo ([model utilities](https://github.com/facebookresearch/WinQ/blob/main/models/utils_quant.py)). No model runs were performed. The source release is an implementation but its demo launch recipe cannot automatically be assumed to be identical to every paper experiment.

Additional flip-control context checked: [Helwegen et al., "Latent Weights Do Not Exist", 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02107) on latent sign-flip inertia, and [Noise-shaped Sampling and Annealing, arXiv 2610.22621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22621), where looser flip settings can destroy 130M ternary continued-pretraining quality. This is not a direct binary W1/g128 comparison; it helps frame the optimization control question.

### 4B. What WinQ says goes wrong

Quantization-aware training with a full-precision latent weight `W` and quantized forward weight `Q(W)` often stalls. WinQ estimates Hessian spectra and interprets the plateau as many nearly flat directions near saddle regions, particularly at 1–2 bits. Its proposed remedies are:

1. **Periodic latent-state resetting:** at selected steps, interpolate the latent `W` toward its deployed quantization `Q(W)` rather than allow it to drift indefinitely away from the discrete code points. The paper writes `W_new = (1-alpha)W + alpha Q(W)`.
2. **Noise-shaped optimization:** sample small Gaussian perturbations to quantized training weights before/within the quantization surrogate, to change the effective local curvature and aid optimization. The deployment format does not store the perturbation.

The method is designed for **ordinary full-precision latent QAT**, not for directly optimizing a file containing only frozen binary signs and scales. Source: [WinQ paper §3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17471), [released quantized linear implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/WinQ/blob/main/models/utils_quant.py).

### 4C. Measured Qwen results, rather than headline gains

WinQ performs QAT on **Llama3-1B, Llama3-3B, Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B**, usually using **FineWebEdu**, up to roughly **20 billion token positions** and **240,000 optimizer steps**, with W1/W2 and various activation quantizers. **Embeddings and output projections remain outside the paper's uniformly quantized target**, and its weight scales are not the user's fixed one FP16 per 128 elements for every matrix. Its benchmark suite is **eight zero-shot QA tasks**, not PrismML's ten-task suite. Source: [WinQ §4 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17471), [FineWebEdu dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-edu).

**Appendix Table 13, Qwen3-1.7B** (lower WikiText2 PPL better, higher average eight-QA accuracy better):

| Model | W/A | WikiText2 PPL | Eight-task average |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| FP16 reference | 16/16 | 16.2 | 58.2 |
| ParetoQ | 1/8 | 46.5 | **42.3** |
| **WinQ** | **1/8** | **45.9** | **42.4** |
| ParetoQ | 2/8 | 22.2 | 47.8 |
| **WinQ** | **2/8** | **21.8** | **48.2** |

Source: [WinQ Appendix Table 13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17471). The **W1** result is **+0.1 absolute average QA point**, corresponding to **about +0.17 teacher-retention percentage point**, vastly below the +5 threshold. The W2 result is +0.4 average point, also below +5. These results do not replicate on the user's 1.125-bpw whole-model format. In the main-text figure the Qwen1.7B ParetoQ and WinQ W1 accuracy are both rounded to **42.2**; the appendix's more detailed table reports **42.3** and **42.4**. The malformed/internally inconsistent FP0.6B line in Appendix Table 13 should not be used for a clean scaling ratio.

The paper also reports **faster approach to matched language-model loss**, including up to 4× in some settings, with low implementation overhead. That is **optimization throughput**, not a measured +5 point benchmark improvement at an equal large training budget. The strongest headline gains are not the small Qwen W1 comparison. Sources: [WinQ abstract, Figures 5–7 and Appendix B.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17471).

### 4D. A reproducibility-sensitive paper/code discrepancy

There is a material discrepancy that should be addressed **before** copying the method into 1 Bit Summit's training pipeline.

- The [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17471) defines the reset `W_new = (1-alpha)*W + alpha*Q(W)`.
- The [released callback, line 43](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/WinQ/main/utils/reinitialize_callback.py), implements `module.weight.data.mul_(alpha).add_(quantized_weight * (1 - alpha))`, namely `alpha*W + (1-alpha)*Q(W)`.
- The [example script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/WinQ/main/scripts/1_run_train.sh) passes a reset-alpha field, with a default demonstrated value `0.2`. If the **same numeric alpha** is used, the code applies an **80% move toward the quantized value**, whereas the paper formula would mean **20%**. They agree after substituting `alpha_code=1-alpha_paper`; no performance conclusion follows until the actual experiment launcher and hyperparameters are reconciled.
- The demo script is a one-process, local training job with a short reset interval; the paper's Qwen1.7B table specifies a reset interval around **80,000 steps** with a different alpha/noise pairing. Thus the demo itself is not a faithful full reproduction recipe for that table.

This is directly relevant to optimization stability: an apparently innocuous coefficient mismatch can change sign-flip inertia and the frequency of code transitions by a large amount. It does **not** prove the published results wrong.

### 4E. Can WinQ start from an already binary model?

Not directly as published. WinQ maintains **floating-point latent tensors** and takes gradients through quantization surrogates. A packed 1.125-bpw file lacks those latents. A binary file can be **used to initialize** a new latent optimizer state (for example set each latent to `sign × group scale`), but that choice makes some signs hard to move and loses the original FP16-magnitude information. The full FP16 teacher can restore original magnitudes *if available*; that is a **training-time** state and not an inference-storage violation, but its memory and generation budget must be counted. Without access to the original pretrained teacher, a separate latent-state initialization must be justified and independently compared.

WinQ's frequent resets might encourage signs to stick rather than move in the helpful locations. Its Gaussian noise may promote exploration but also create destructive flips. Therefore the right property to measure is **beneficial sign changes per unit of teacher-aligned loss**, not simply "more signs flipped" or "less latent-grid drift". For g128 groups, shared-scale updates can change 128 deployed magnitudes without changing any sign; track these mechanisms separately.

Other related but non-equivalent methods include [Bop / latent-sign inertia](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02107) and [noise-shaped discrete sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22621), which gives an explicit handle on code-change behavior but whose language-model STE comparison is on a ternary ~130M checkpoint with continuous interface weights. Neither paper provides a published matched-runtime +5 point strict-binary Qwen result.

**Five-point probability for WinQ-style reset plus noise on fixed 1.125-bpw Qwen1.7B:** **before 16%, after 7%**. The method is empirically real at one-bit weight precision and correct scale, but the **best matching Qwen W1 benchmark gain is only 0.1 point** on a different test suite after a very large budget. **Cheapest discriminating proof:** compare standard STE, reset-only, noise-only and combined reset/noise using exactly the same strict-g128 initialized model and matched total token/FLOP budgets. Explicitly verify both alpha conventions before training. Record sign transitions by original magnitude, group-scale drift and task-level correct-to-wrong transitions. If a change is only perplexity or 0.1 QA point with no net five-point fixed-suite gain, it is at most a secondary training-efficiency optimization.

## 5. Cross-item synthesis and new hypotheses

### 5A. Ranked opportunities under the fixed product criterion

| Research avenue | My before → after probability of +5pp | Evidence actually at 1.7B and strict g128 whole-model W1? | Primary risk |
|---|---:|---|---|
| On-policy dynamic-KL teacher on **student's own generated prefixes** | **15% → 25%** | **No**; Qwen3-1.7B W2/W3, asymmetric groups | Huge inference supervision cost; code/math data imbalance; sign-only optimizer may not track teacher |
| Forensic-informed body compensation around **largely rounded tied interface** | **8% → 11%** | First-gen Bonsai *weights observed*, no reproduced conversion | Fingerprint is non-identifying; requires matched conversion |
| Binary adaptation of discrepancy-guided teacher-logit rounding | **12% → 9%** | **No**; tested 3–4.5 bit at 3.8B/8B | Binary grid, group-scale coupling and model size violate assumptions |
| WinQ latent resets plus noise | **16% → 7%** | **No**; W1 body at 1.7B, not tied interface/g128 | Published Qwen W1 accuracy gain tiny after substantial compute |

The strongest practical opportunity is **not a new quantization formula by itself**. It is to integrate a diverse, student-prefix behavioral objective with a **strict-format binary optimizer** that can actually alter the few signs and scales most responsible for decision errors. One part without the other risks spending far more tokens to produce a lower KL but no additional correct answers.

### 5B. Three blind spots worth resolving before spending the 1.7B training budget

**Blind spot 1: judging a body-only optimizer against a whole-model target.** ThakiCloud's most cited fingerprints exclude the tied embedding, whereas Kalaris indicates that Bonsai1.7B's tied interface is near direct rounding. This is a **different structural strategy** from treating every matrix as equally trainable. Compare fixed rounded interface plus aggressively compensated body against joint interface/body sign training. Both final models still satisfy exact g128; the difference is the optimization path, not a precision exception. Sources: [ThakiCloud code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/main/scripts/bonsai_repro.py), [Kalaris](https://kalarislabs.com/blog/prismml-bonsai-team-1-bit-reverse-engineering).

**Blind spot 2: the data objective and the model's error distribution are inseparable.** OnQ's multi-domain OPD gains can be lost on code if trained math-only. DiscQuant's math-enriched calibration improves GSM8K while worsening PIQA. The user scorecard includes MATH, code, BFCL and instructions. A single-domain curriculum may add 5 retention points in one task and lose them in others. Use predeclared task-mix quotas, disjoint train/eval pools and report lost/gained teacher-correct items. Sources: [OnQ indexed tables](https://bittide.aicompass.dev/article/db0642b6-3533-42ce-9def-2417956be2dd), [DiscQuant §5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06417).

**Blind spot 3: zero inference bits does not mean zero conversion cost or runtime invariance.** Extra FP16 latent states, teacher passes, student rollouts, rotations and scale-fitting caches may all be acceptable **during conversion**, but a learned transformation that needs an unfoldable runtime operator is not the same final format. A mathematical optimizer may win at budget-matched processed tokens but lose after teacher rollout generation and checkpoint selection are counted. Maintain separate **training-only memory**, **total conversion compute**, **final inference operations** and **deployed storage** ledgers.

### 5C. My independent proposal: minimize corrected-answer cost, not just language-model loss

Define a **teacher-correct error bank** from the *fixed held-out scorecard*: for each task, identify instances where the FP16 teacher is correct and the initial W1 model wrong. Do **not** train on held-out benchmark text. Instead label analogous independent public training instances by failure mechanism: early answer reversal, repeated reasoning loop, invalid tool-schema arguments, lost factual recall, incorrect code execution, or instruction parser mismatch. Train with mixed student-prefix teacher supervision from **nonoverlapping sources** and a code-change control (for example an inertial latent rule) that directs updates to maintain task breadth. Check the real scorecard only at predeclared checkpoints. At each milestone record: new correct, newly broken, tokens/FLOPs/teacher generations, stored bits and runtime cost. This is a proposal, not an established result; it does **not** require changing the 1.125-bpw deployment constraint.

This combines the most promising direct behavioral evidence (OnQ) with the strongest forensic warning (function-space changes rather than weight-space fidelity) and avoids promoting WinQ's modest W1 PPL improvements into an unmeasured capability win. A +5pp trophy remains **unearned** until same-model, same-suite ten-benchmark retention is measured.
