# 1 Bit Summit: Independent Review of Knowledge Loss, Discrete Optimizers, and Pretrained Conversion Routes

Research basis: public primary sources available on 24 November 2026. Independent review. No model was trained or evaluated for this document.

## 1. Scope, source-access record, and the decision rule

### 1A. Two qualifications before interpreting the research

1. The specified target is not a generic "one-bit" or "approximately one-bit" model. It is **128 signs plus one FP16 group scale**, exactly **18 bytes per 128 matrix weights**, or **1.125 bits per weight**, including the **input embedding and language-model output matrix**, with a tied matrix represented once if tied. A method that keeps the head in FP16, stores a per-row magnitude vector, uses two signs per weight, or adds inference-time normalization is a scientific lead, **not a conforming result**. The distinction matters in [OneBit](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295), [BinaryLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974), and [BitDistill](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998).
2. The next five **percentage points of benchmark retention** are not five raw accuracy points or five percent more correct answers on one task. With PrismML's stated 10-task teacher mean of 58.24, the threshold is **29.12 total raw benchmark-score points** across ten tasks, because `0.05 × 58.24 × 10 = 29.12`. PrismML's stated MMLU-Redux and GPQA Diamond losses total **35.4** points (`66.8 - 43.2 + 32.5 - 20.7`). Reclaiming the whole five-point milestone from those two tasks alone would require **82.3%** of their combined losses. Source: [PrismML Bonsai release repository and whitepaper list](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo). The benchmark entries are supplied by the project owner; the derived arithmetic is exact.

These facts impose an important constraint on any proposed "knowledge repair": improving MMLU-Redux and GPQA is useful, but an exclusive focus on them is a concentrated bet. It is not the cheapest route to five points unless the repair also helps other benchmarks or recovers most of these losses.

## 2. Item 1: What is factual knowledge loss at very low precision?

### 2A. Distinguish facts missing from facts inaccessible at the final decision

A multiple-choice failure can arise at several different levels. These are **mechanistic hypotheses to discriminate**, not observed frequencies in Qwen3-1.7B:

- **Representational erosion:** internal projections no longer preserve features carrying a factual association, and the correct option genuinely loses probability on many paraphrases. This is plausible after large perturbations to FFN projections and residual streams, but no attribution of the 23.6 MMLU and 11.8 GPQA raw-score deficits to this mechanism is provided by the [PrismML benchmark whitepaper repository](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo).
- **Decision-boundary displacement:** the model still assigns substantial probability to the right answer but the wrong option wins. In that case, a narrow change in the final logits or later representation can restore the answer without reconstructing all original internal features. Teacher-relative KL is not sufficient to identify the effect on a particular choice. This is an **inference**; the source papers use differing losses and benchmarks rather than performing the required paired item audit, as can be seen in [OneBit](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295) and [BinaryLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974).
- **Output-head instability or finite-precision pathology:** a token's logit or output-head row becomes excessive or poorly resolved, making the final choice fail even when some internal information is present. This mechanism is **directly demonstrated under a controlled synthetic setting**, not established for MMLU or GPQA, by [*Linger and Lose*, Section 8 and Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902).
- **Instruction, format, or reasoning-execution error:** a benchmark answer may fail even if some factual material is available. Distinguishing option selection, long-form rationale, instruction compliance and factual recall requires separate scoring. The existence of task-specific breakdowns in the [PrismML benchmarks](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo) does not itself isolate these mechanisms.

**Inference:** The basic failure taxonomy matters because training on more factual text repairs representational omissions differently from calibrating a decision boundary, fixing output-head stability, or repairing a generation template. Treating every wrong MC answer as "lost knowledge" prejudges the answer.

**Independent diagnostic research, with limited reading.** The published ACL Findings 2026 abstract of [*From Signal Degradation to Computation Collapse*](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1162/) distinguishes **signal degradation**, where the computation remains operational but accumulates error, from **computation collapse**, where essential components fail early; its authors state that targeted, training-free repair helps the former but not the latter. This is **peer-reviewed abstract-level evidence** against assuming that more careful calibration will fix every 2-bit failure; I did not read its full PDF and cannot assign its specific experimental sizes or repair gains here. Separately, the available description of [*PRISM: a diagnostic framework for parametric recall degradation*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10791-026-10580-5) reports Qwen2.5-3B/7B results suggesting closed-book parametric recall deteriorates faster than use of contradictory in-context evidence, and attributes much of the perturbation effect to MLP quantization. That source's full article was inaccessible in this review and **its models are bigger and its formats not our strict 1-bit setup**. Together, these two descriptions reinforce the need to distinguish lost memory, corrupted computation and shifted choice probabilities, rather than declaring them identical.

### 2B. What *Linger and Lose* actually established

[Primary full text, Sections 3 to 9 and Appendices A to I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902).

**Design.** Four GPT-2-style decoder models from **2.5M to 50M**, trained **from scratch** on synthetic six-attribute biographies at controlled knowledge load. The **body** uses INT8, INT4, INT3 or **ternary**, but the input embedding and output head are **untied INT4 in every low-bit run**. Group size is **64**, not 128. Activations are BF16. Thus the study is **not** a pretrained 1-bit g128 conversion and **not** a natural-language MMLU or GPQA study. Sources: [methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902), [Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902).

**Damage.** The authors measure synthetic knowledge-capacity bits per parameter, not benchmark accuracy. Under heavily loaded, cosine-schedule ternary training, the ratio of quantized capacity to matched FP16 is extremely small in some larger runs; different definitions of the clipped capacity estimator change the severity. A conspicuous failure is a particular birth-month attribute that remains near chance while the corresponding output-head rows grow rapidly. In one collapsing run, the average norm of the 12 month rows rose from **18.5 to 41.7**, while other rows declined from **2.6 to 2.2**. The largest affected logit rose from **431 to 11,879**. On collapsed checkpoints, FP32-versus-BF16 forward accumulation can drastically change measured loss on the affected token, indicating a numerical-resolution component as well as a training failure. Source: [Section 8 and Appendices G to I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902).

**Repairs with limits.** A **10% warmup-stable-decay cooldown** and reducing the output-head learning rate to one-tenth could avert or reduce the observed collapse, depending on seed and setting. At full load, the 25M ternary cell's reported capacity climbed from around **0.12 to 0.16** in collapsing cosine runs to **0.423** with the cooldown; at 50M, it reached **0.382** under cooldown. These values are synthetic *R* units, not percent task retention or +5 benchmark points. The authors show that making the head FP16 helps some runs, but does not prevent all collapses. Most directly relevant, an FP16-pretrained-to-ternary fine-tune does **not** reproduce the warm-start success automatically: a reported fine-tuning variant quickly destroys much of its inherited synthetic knowledge. Sources: [Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902), [WSD comparisons and Section 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902).

**Interpretation.** The paper is strong evidence for a hidden variable, **dwell time at a damaging learning-rate/quantization regime**, and for an output-head control point. It is not evidence that every MMLU error means the correct fact was erased from internal memory. The head is **INT4 and untied**, so simply adopting the demonstrated head fix in the tied, completely binary Qwen would be an unjustified transfer. The useful transplant is *gradient and head-row control*, but it must respect a single binary shared matrix at deployment.

The paper record therefore supports three qualified conclusions. First, **a huge task-specific discriminative drop can be recovered** at 0.6B and 1.7B using ternary with task data ([BitDistill](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998)). Second, maintaining a low perplexity or decent overall accuracy does not ensure ARC or other multiple-choice categories recover ([BinaryLLM Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974), [OneBit Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295)). Third, the **published cited evidence does not yet attribute the Qwen3-1.7B MMLU-Redux and GPQA losses to a unique mechanism**, and none of these papers measures the exact whole-model g128 binary interface.

### 2D. Cost to other abilities, not just headline recovery

- BitDistill tunes task-specific FP16 teachers and students using corresponding downstream training data, so its recovered MNLI/QNLI/SST-2 outcomes do **not** prove preservation on MATH-500, code, instruction following, or unseen knowledge after specializing. The paper's evaluation scope is classification and summarization ([Sections 4.1–4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998)). Cross-task regression on the ten Princeton/PrismML-style tasks is **unmeasured**.
- OneBit's 1.3B OPT model loses **19.47 HellaSwag points** (53.73 to 34.26) while **gaining 1.77 BoolQ points** (57.68 to 59.45). A successful repair for one category is not proof of balanced improvement ([OneBit Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295)).
- Linger and Lose's output-head control can restrain pathological growth while also shrinking head row magnitudes generally, and the result depends on load/schedule. There is no zero-regression guarantee ([Section 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902)).

### 2E. Answer, five-point probability, and cheapest discriminating evidence

**Answer:** At ≤2 bits and small scale, the clearest demonstrated recovery is **task-specialized ternary distillation**, not recovered factual memory in our strict binary representation. The strongest new mechanism from *Linger and Lose* is **output-head/precision and learning-rate instability that can masquerade as knowledge loss**. There may be multiple causes in Qwen; a single "train on facts" intervention assumes too much. Sources: [BitDistill Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998), [Linger and Lose Section 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902).

**Judged chance of gaining at least five overall retention percentage points from a primarily knowledge/MC repair, measured on Qwen3-1.7B and at exact g128 binary:** **before reading 18%; after reading 12%**. This is subjective and conditioned on a working binary baseline; evidence that ternary task-specialized training repairs MNLI raises optimism about repairability, but the 82.3% concentration hurdle and tied-head mismatch lower confidence in a standalone five-point outcome. A combined effect across task families would increase the chance, but that is a different intervention.

**Unknowns:** How many of the teacher-correct/student-wrong MMLU and GPQA answers remain recoverable by option-margin adjustment? Is the error localized to output/last blocks or distributed across FFNs? Does FP16 logit precision change the label of the binary model on the same fixed prompt? Can a training-only asymmetric gradient for the tied head control its output-role instability without breaking the lookup role?

**Cheapest decisive evidence:** Without modifying the model, create a **paired, immutable item-level audit** on existing held-out MMLU/GPQA prompts, extracting teacher and binary option logits, margins, logit scales and numerical-precision sensitivity, plus several unseen paraphrases. Count teacher-correct/student-wrong, teacher-wrong/student-correct and their confidence margins, and localize the earliest layer where option identity changes. This is an evaluation/diagnostic proposal, not a claim that the mechanism is already identified. Dataset items used to select repairs must not reenter the final scoreboard.

## 3. Item 2: Is there a discrete-weight optimizer that beats STE at language-model scale?

### 3A. The question confounds two distinct algorithm classes

**JointQ** is an **offline post-training quantization search** over discrete codes and their scales. It does not optimize a language-model training loss over SGD steps and its core comparisons are against GPTQ, not STE. **ΣΔ-Sampler** is a **training optimizer** that updates discrete codes directly, and does contain a small-model STE head-to-head, but on a **ternary** already-low-bit checkpoint. Directly treating their headline results as equivalent evidence of a better binary training optimizer is a category error. Sources: [JointQ Sections 2–4](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384), [ΣΔ-Sampler Section 2.4, Table S5](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621).

### 3B. JointQ: precise mechanism and what wins

[Primary PDF and appendices](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384), [code location](https://github.com/FujitsuResearch/OneCompression/tree/main/onecomp/quantizer/jointq), with incomplete independent code audit as noted in Section 1B.

**Mechanism.** For an output row of weights `w`, replace a fixed-scale nearest-integer decision with a coupled optimization of `q` (integer codes) and `s` (group scales), using the activation Gram/Hessian `H` to minimize `((w - Σ_g s_g q_g) H (w - Σ_g s_g q_g)^T)`. The algorithm starts from GPTQ and clipping-based candidates, refits group scales jointly, selects the lower-loss initial row, and then searches individual code changes while **re-solving the affected scale and accounting for other groups**. It adds layer-specific diagonal stabilization and retry rules. [Sections 3.1–3.9, Algorithms 1 and 2](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384). Its monotonicity result certifies improvement of a *local regularized objective*, explicitly **not** downstream accuracy ([Proposition 3.6](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384)).

**What was tested:** pretrained Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, Qwen3-8B-Base and Qwen3-14B-Base with **W4G128, W3G128, per-channel W3/W4** and extra **W2G32**; not 1-bit, not Qwen3-1.7B, and not the tied head ([Section 4](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384)). Calibration was 1,024 C4 sequences of 2,048 tokens for main comparisons, with sensitivity studies on less data ([Section 4.1 and Appendix G](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384)).

**Representative W2G32 results** (six zero-shot tasks averaged, not our ten-task score):

| Pretrained model | FP16 | GPTQ | JointQ | Net improvement vs GPTQ | Quality caveat |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Llama-2-7B | 70.38% | 36.07% | 49.27% | +13.20 task points | Still 21.11 below teacher |
| Qwen3-8B | 74.03% | 40.63% | 57.55% | +16.92 task points | Still 16.48 below teacher; WikiText PPL **45.98** vs FP16 **9.73** |

Source: [JointQ Table 4, W2G32](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384). The stronger result belongs to an **extremely damaged two-bit GPTQ baseline at larger size**. It is not evidence that adding JointQ to already-strong STE binary training yields +13 to +17 points.

**Ablation and baseline correction:** JointQ improves 14/16 accuracy comparisons over **vanilla GPTQ** across four main higher-bit settings, but against GPTQ with both activation ordering and MSE scale search (`GPTQ*`), it wins only **1 of 8 G128** accuracy comparisons (and all 8 per-channel ones). This greatly limits confidence that *groupwise* scale/code coupling alone is a major unexplored advantage when strong initialization is already available. At least three models' regularization choices were screened on evaluation data used in the reported results, making parts of the table optimistic as unbiased out-of-sample estimates. [JointQ Appendix E, Table 8, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384).

**Transfer to our format:** The **mathematical coupling of exact sign flips and an FP16 scale per group** transfers. Integer codebooks of 3 or 4 bits do not. One sign flip at 1 bit has a much larger finite change, and a positive scale has only one degree of freedom for 128 signs. The useful published component is evaluating a candidate flip **after** optimizing all affected scales, rather than keeping scales fixed; there is no demonstrated five-point end-to-end effect at Qwen3-1.7B ([JointQ Section 3](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384)).

### 3C. ΣΔ-Sampler: true alternative to latent-weight STE

[Author-hosted full paper and supplement](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621); [stated code repository](https://github.com/aimlab-wustl/SD-SA), which could not be independently inspected.

**Mechanism.** The sampler maintains **binary or ternary codes directly**, a noise-shaped feedback integrator and an annealed acceptance threshold. In contrast, STE retains latent floating weights whose signs emerge after crossing zero, which can leave binary codes locked during short resumed training. The sampler regulates the **fraction of changed codes per update**; its update-rate cap is an important hyperparameter. Continuous scales and other continuous parameters are optimized separately. Source: [ΣΔ-Sampler Section 2 and Supplement S5](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621).

**Language-model demonstrations:** MatMul-Free LMs of **370M, 1.3B and 2.7B**, **ternary** recurrent/linear units, trained **from scratch**, 2,000 steps with 532,480 processed positions each, about **1.065 billion processed token positions** over a fixed FineWeb-Edu shard. **Embeddings/output head remain continuous**, unlike our target. On 1.3B the final-100-step training loss is **5.9684**, but there is no matched 1.3B STE-versus-sampler language-model task benchmark. [Table 4, S4](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621).

**The direct STE comparison** is instead continued pretraining a **130M already ternary** MatMul-Free LM checkpoint for **100 steps**. All arms use the same data and continuous-parameter optimizer, but the authors identify a **different initial loss due to different scale initialization**, confounding a pure optimizer-only conclusion. On held-out evaluation loss: [Table S5](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621):

| Update rule | Initial eval loss | Final eval loss | Final gap vs STE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ternary STE | 3.1721 | **3.1644** | Baseline |
| ΣΔ-Sampler, cap `1e-5` | 3.1951 | **3.0800** | -0.0844 |
| ΣΔ-Sampler, cap `1e-4` | 3.1950 | **3.0241** | **-0.1403** |
| ΣΔ-Sampler, cap `1e-3` | 3.1951 | **3.0913** | -0.0731 |
| ΣΔ-Sampler, cap `1e-1` | 3.1950 | **7.1105** | **+3.9461**, catastrophic |

**Why the negative control matters:** The same optimizer family can be substantially better or far worse depending on flip budget. Its best from-scratch cap for 370M is not the best for 1.3B; the best cap also reverses between from-scratch and resumed training. Even the paper cautions against a universal default. Source: [Supplement S5.1–S5.2](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621). The reported memory/DRAM advantages exclude activation storage and include analytical transaction estimates, rather than an independent guarantee of lower full training cost ([Supplement S6](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621)).

**Transfer to our format:** Direct sampling of sign codes with an **explicit per-layer/per-group flip-rate budget** is compatible as a training-only optimizer. A scale must still be fitted, clamped/rounded to FP16 on deployment and coupled to sign moves. Ternary's switch through zero is not available to strict binary weights. There is no evidence yet that removing zeros and quantizing the tied output matrix preserves the sampler's advantage. The best supported starting point is **a pretrained model converted to a working strict-binary student**, because this is the closest analogue to the favorable 130M checkpoint continuation. The paper does not establish that it beats STE at 1.3B pretrained-to-binary.

### 3D. Answer and probability

**Answer:** There is a **promising small-model ternary language-model optimizer with better held-out loss than STE** under one controlled resumed-training comparison, and there is a strong **offline PTQ refinement** over weak baselines at two bits. There is **not yet demonstrated evidence in these sources** that either beats strong **whole-model g128 binary STE** at 1.7B while also improving the specified ten-benchmark retention score. This statement is scoped to the inspected [ΣΔ-Sampler](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621) and [JointQ](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384) sources, not a claim about every paper ever published.

**Judged probability that a discrete-optimizer improvement by itself yields at least +5 retention percentage points on the exact format:** **before reading 15%; after reading 10%**. The positive evidence supports trying a safeguarded search, but the head-to-head is too small and ternary, and JointQ's G128 control is substantially weaker than its vanilla-GPTQ headline. The failure risk of an uncontrolled sign-change rate is directly measured.

**Cheapest discriminating evidence:** One **single matched comparison** on the same frozen pretrained decoder and strict all-matrix binary start, same training data, measured processed tokens and total compute: (a) tuned STE with restored latents, (b) direct-sign noise-shaped updates with a tightly bounded flip budget, (c) scale-refitted exact binary coordinate improvements initialized from the same state. No additional deployed parameters; same packed 18-byte groups and tied head. Inspect total compute, untouched item-level accuracy and lost-vs-gained decisions. A candidate wins only if it improves the scored task suite, not simply its calibration loss. This proof must not use the final ten-task test set for optimizer/hyperparameter selection.

## 4. Item 3: Published pretrained-to-ternary and pretrained-to-binary routes

### 4A. BitDistill, Microsoft: a strong task-recovery result with a different model contract

[Complete paper and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998); [linked Microsoft BitNet repository](https://github.com/microsoft/BitNet), whose inspected README is for inference, **not a verified complete BitDistill training recipe**.

**Starting point:** Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B pretrained full-precision models, compared with **task-fine-tuned FP16 teachers**. **Precision:** per-tensor **ternary** weight codes (round/clip `W / mean|W|`), 8-bit activations, STE. Adds **SubLN** before attention output projection and FFN output projection. This changes inference architecture, so retained norm parameters must count in deployed costs. The paper does not provide a complete tensor-by-tensor packed checkpoint audit certifying that a tied LM head was ternarized, let alone g128 binarized. [Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998).

**Three stages, exact published losses:**

1. **Architecture stabilization:** Insert SubLN before the two output projections. No standalone training loss for inserting SubLN. [Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998).
2. **Continued pretraining:** ordinary next-token CE `L_CT=-Σ log P(c_t|c_<t)` on **10 billion FALCON corpus tokens**, **not teacher KL** at this stage. [Section 3.2 and Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998).
3. **Supervised task-specific distillation:** `L = L_CE(task labels) + λ KL(teacher_τ || student_τ) + γ L_attention_relation`, where the attention term matches pairwise self-relations of normalized **Q, K and V** features, usually on a *single layer*. Temperature **5**; classification `λ=10`, `γ=1e5`; summarization `λ=1`, `γ=1e3`; batch **32**, maximum sequence **512**; LR and training epochs selected by a greedy search rather than fixed published values. Teacher is an FP16 model **fine-tuned on the same downstream task**. [Section 3.3, Algorithm 1, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998).

**Every Qwen3 at-or-below 1.7B classification result** in the published Table 1, beside its task-specific FP16 teacher:

| Size and task | FP16-SFT teacher | Direct ternary SFT | BitDistill ternary | Retention of BitDistill vs task teacher |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen3-0.6B MNLI | 88.01 | 74.09 | **88.17** | 100.18% |
| Qwen3-0.6B QNLI | 93.72 | 78.32 | **93.66** | 99.94% |
| Qwen3-0.6B SST-2 | 94.21 | 79.92 | **94.30** | 100.10% |
| Qwen3-1.7B MNLI | 89.61 | 75.27 | **89.53** | 99.91% |

| Qwen3-1.7B SST-2 | 95.43 | 81.37 | **95.26** | 99.82% |

Ratios calculated from the published task values, **not our ten-benchmark capability retention**, and not evidence of a general 1-bit Qwen. In particular, the project cannot claim "99% of general ability" from one individually trained MNLI student.

**Ablations:** Table 5 on Qwen3-0.6B MNLI reports ordinary direct ternary SFT **74.09**, +SubLN **76.30**, +SubLN and continued pretraining without task-distillation **86.73**, +SubLN and distillation **without continued pretraining 88.04**, and full three-stage method **88.17**. Thus the last 10B-token stage contributes only **+0.13 MNLI points** when *both* SubLN and task-specific distillation are already present, despite a +10.43 difference in the **different** setting with no task-distillation. In Table 6, adding logit KD alone raises **86.73 -> 87.32**, attention KD alone **86.73 -> 87.67**, both **88.17**. Sources: [Tables 5 and 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998).

**What matters:** Task-relative KD is directly effective at this size; stabilizing normalization and relation matching are plausible modules to transplant. **What does not follow:** That 10B continued-training tokens are always necessary, that learned SubLN is free in a strict format, or that a supervised MNLI recovery transfers to GPQA or MMLU with no catastrophic regression. The authors' own ablation suggests **task data and distillation may be more efficient than a long generic warmup for some narrow tasks**. That is a genuinely important research-priority change, not a claim that the general-conversion problem has been solved.

### 4B. OneBit: factorized signs and magnitudes, not our group-scale codec

[Full paper with Appendix A.1–A.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295), [released training repo](https://github.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit), [quantized linear module source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit/main/transformers/src/transformers/models/bitnet.py), and [SVID checkpoint builder](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit/main/scripts/build_start_ckpt.py).

**Starting point and representation:** pretrained OPT-1.3B and larger models. For each quantized linear matrix, store one matrix of `±1` signs **plus an FP16 input vector and an FP16 output vector**, such that `Y = LN(((X ⊙ g) sign(W)^T) ⊙ h)`. This is a **rank-one factorization of |W|**, initialized by NMF, not the project's 128-weight shared scalar. The code explicitly constructs `input_factor` and per-output `weight_scale`, retains an output LayerNorm, and enumerates conversion of **q/k/v/o/gate/up/down** weights, not the tied embedding/head. A nominal **1.0073 bpw** is computed for a large matrix, but its row/column vector overhead and inference operations differ from g128. Sources: [Sections 3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295), [bitnet.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit/main/transformers/src/transformers/models/bitnet.py), [build_start_ckpt.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit/main/scripts/build_start_ckpt.py).

**Objective:** `L_KD = L_CE(teacher-generated data, student predictions) + α Σ||normalize(h_T)-normalize(h_S)||²`, matching intermediate teacher/student hidden directions. Here `L_CE` is teacher-output KD cross entropy, **not generic supervised benchmark SFT**. Train with Adam `β1=.9, β2=.98`, cosine learning rate, **50 epochs** over **132,000 generated sequences**, each of at most 2,048 tokens. The data are generated by teacher next-token sampling: first three to five tokens greedy, later stochastic; the release README calls the dataset `kd_132k`. The actual total processed tokens are not specified as an independently audited count: **132k × 2,048 × 50 = 13.52B is an upper-bound-style planning product if every segment were full length**, not a measured token count. For OPT-1.3B, LR **4e-4**, α **1.0**. The paper reports no Qwen3-1.7B result. Sources: [Section 4.1, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295), [repository dataset instructions](https://github.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit).

**The only primary result ≤2B:** OPT-1.3B teacher six-task average **54.00**, OneBit **45.46**, relative retention **84.19%**; per-task teacher to OneBit: WinoGrande **59.67 -> 51.14**, HellaSwag **53.73 -> 34.26**, PIQA **72.42 -> 62.57**, BoolQ **57.68 -> 59.45**, ARC-e **50.80 -> 41.25**, ARC-c **29.69 -> 24.06**. WikiText2 perplexity **14.63 -> 25.42**, C4 **14.72 -> 22.95**. [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295). Other OneBit results in the paper are >2B and do not qualify as directly at the target size.

**Ablations:** The strongest quantitative comparison of hidden-state alignment is **on LLaMA-7B**, not ≤2B: KD-only average **45.50**, KD plus hidden-state MSE **51.33** with α=1, **52.05** with α=10; adding attention-score alignment produces **NaN divergence**. This directly **conflicts in direction** with BitDistill's beneficial Q/K/V relation loss, and is a reason **not** to import one paper's distillation weights into another architecture uncritically. Other OneBit ablations on normalization, NMF initialization, and gradient stability appear largely as curves or qualitative descriptions, so exact numeric gains cannot be asserted from the accessible rendered text. Source: [Appendix A.5–A.6 Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295).

**Which ingredients carry over:** NMF/SVID and teacher-hidden matching are useful *training-only initialization and loss* ideas. The extra input/output magnitude vectors and added normalization **do not directly fit** one group scale per 128 signs; converting those vectors to g128 scalars may discard the advantage. OneBit's code-based evidence is especially important because it establishes what is actually stored, beyond the phrase "one bit".

### 4C. BinaryLLM: pretrained progressive sign training, but body-only accounting

[Full paper with Appendices A–C](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974). No complete released training implementation was verified for this review.

**Starting point and codec:** pretrained SmolLM-135M, LLaMA3-1.3B, LLaMA3-3B. The paper explicitly calculates its quoted approximately **1.01 bpw only over transformer-body linear weights** and excludes the token embedding and output head. Therefore the published model is **not a compliant full-model binary conversion** under 1 Bit Summit's definition. [Section 4.2, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974).

**Three mechanisms:** (i) **binary-aware initial channel scaling**, optimizing a scaling variable for an autoregressive objective for **50 calibration steps** with core weights frozen; (ii) **progressive training** with `F(x,t)=tanh(tx)/tanh(t)` in both forward and backward, rather than forward sign plus backward STE, over **20 data chunks/phases** with schedule `t(c)=1.3 exp(0.22c)-1.3`; and (iii) **dual scaling** `S_l S_a F(W/S_a,t)`, where analytical absmean `S_a` and a separately learned `S_l` multiply. At final inference, the scales merge and `F` is replaced by sign. The method uses **autoregressive next-token training**, not teacher logit KD or MSE. [Sections 3.2–3.4 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974).

**Data and training:** **RedPajama**, sequence length **2,048**, batch **128**, AdamW, cosine LR **1e-4 -> 2e-6**, weight decay **0.1**; paper explicitly cites **20 billion processed tokens** for SmolLM-135M, while a separate total for the LLaMA3-1.3B main result is not unambiguously stated in the accessible main text. Its other 50B reference is for **Pythia-70M ablation sampling**, not evidence of a uniform 1.3B budget. Do not invent a 1.3B training-token figure. Source: [Section 4.1–4.2, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974).

**Own-teacher results ≤2B, main Table 2:**

| Pretrained source | FP16 seven-task avg | BinaryLLM avg | Retention | WikiText2 PPL FP16 -> binary | C4 PPL FP16 -> binary |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| SmolLM-135M | **49.1** | **42.8** | **87.17%** | 17.6 -> 26.8 | 22.1 -> 28.1 |
| LLaMA3-1.3B | **55.2** | **48.7** | **88.22%** | 9.8 -> 14.7 | 14.0 -> 18.4 |

Source: [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974). Note that the 135M ablation in **Table 3** reports **42.3** on a separate run/baseline condition, so it must not be silently substituted for Table 2's **42.8**. Appendix B contains an apparent teacher WikiText2/C4 discrepancy for LLaMA3-1B relative to Table 2; the table above uses the main matched Table 2 values, not the contradictory appendix baseline. [Table 3 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974).

**Important task regressions at small size:** SmolLM teacher HellaSwag **42.6 -> 31.2** and ARC-e **56.3 -> 41.5** (main Table 2); LLaMA3-1.3B HellaSwag **63.7 -> 49.6**, ARC-e **60.4 -> 47.4**, ARC-c **36.4 -> 26.0**. These costs are not captured by the 88% seven-task average alone. [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974).

**Ablation:** On SmolLM-135M under the Table 4 progressive comparison, vanilla binary **41.0**, IR-Net **41.1**, proposed progressive rule **42.0** seven-task avg. This isolates an approximately **+1 task-average point** advantage against vanilla, **not** +5 points of our teacher-normalized ten-task retention, and not proof that longer training was unnecessary. On Pythia-70M, the dual scale reduces mean perplexity relative to single-scale alternatives, but those numbers are from a smaller teacher and do not establish capability retention. [Table 4, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974).

**Which ingredients carry over:** Training-only progressive backward alignment and the concept of analytical scale plus learned correction are compatible *in principle* with per-g128 signs/scales. But the actual published body-only implementation uses its own scale topology, training budgets, and a frozen high-precision interface. Moving it to an all-binary tied input/output head is an unvalidated architectural change, not an accounting adjustment.

### 4D. Cross-paper synthesis, five-point probability, cheapest discriminating evidence

| Method | Target codec compliant? | Pretrained start? | ≤2B own-teacher result? | Most useful isolated ingredient | Principal disqualifier for a direct scoreboard claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BitDistill | **No**, ternary, 8-bit activations, extra SubLN | **Yes** | **Yes**, 0.6B/1.7B downstream-task SFT | Output KL + selected attention Q/K/V relational distillation, task data | Task-specialized result is not general benchmark retention; tied head not audited |
| OneBit | **No**, two FP16 vectors/matrix and output normalization | **Yes** | **Yes**, OPT-1.3B 45.46/54.00 | Magnitude-factorized initialization, teacher-directed intermediate-state alignment | Binary head not included in the inspected conversion module list; magnitude vectors violate exact representation |
| BinaryLLM | **No**, body-only bit accounting | **Yes** | **Yes**, SmolLM-135M and LLaMA3-1.3B | Consistent progressive gradients, analytical plus learned scale | 20B tokens at 135M; exact tied all-binary interface not tested |
| JointQ | **No**, W2G32 best low-bit result | **Yes**, PTQ | **No**, evaluated 7B+ | Exact discrete-code and group-scale coupling | Not STE comparison; at 2-bit and larger models |
| ΣΔ-Sampler | **No**, ternary body with continuous head | **Yes for 0.13B resumed training**, from-scratch larger models | **Yes**, small STE validation-loss comparison, no teacher benchmark | Noise-shaped sign-update budget during recovery | Not 1-bit, head continuous, strong cap sensitivity |

Sources: [BitDistill](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998), [OneBit](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11295), [BinaryLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974), [JointQ](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384), [ΣΔ-Sampler](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621).

**Answer:** The evidence does **not** support importing an entire published recipe unchanged. It supports a narrow but potentially powerful strategy: derive a *legal* strict-binary initial state, then apply **teacher-directed multi-level supervision** while controlling **training-only gradients and discrete sign-motion**, and never preserve extra vectors/norms at inference unless their costs are included and allowed. The strongest numeric precedent for recovering task ability at 1.7B is BitDistill's ternary classification, while the strongest near-binary pretrained body-only performance evidence is BinaryLLM. Neither has demonstrated the target's tied, g128-binary output. This is a synthesis, not a reproduced result.

**Judged probability of +5 retention percentage points on Qwen3-1.7B from adapting a *combination* of the best ingredients under the exact codec:** **before reading 20%; after reading 26%**. It increases because BitDistill's targeted-loss ablation is more favorable than a naive reading of its 10B-token budget, and because OneBit and BinaryLLM identify different compensatory gradients, but it remains low due to strict-head exclusions and incompatible parameterizations. The probability is not a statistically estimated value; it is conditional on a viable initial strict-binary student, fixed suite, and measured baseline. It is **not additive** to probabilities in Sections 2 and 3.

**Unknowns:** whether relation losses need an added norm layer; whether selected-layer hidden or Q/K/V matching helps **binary** rather than **ternary** recovery; whether OneBit's stable magnitude factors can be **algebraically folded** into permissible exact symmetries or represented by fixed g128 scales with no extra parameters; whether BinaryLLM's progressive rule is beneficial once the tied head is also binary; whether data specialization breaks other nine tasks.

**Cheapest decisive evidence:** A **legal-format interface-first** comparison, keeping every matrix g128 binary and head tied, then adding **one training-only supervision component at a time** to the same baseline: output KL, hidden normalized agreement, one-layer Q/K/V relation loss, and progressive gradient rule. Compare actual benchmark retention and loss-vs-gain transitions on untouched held-out items at equal **total compute**, and audit the packed weights. Start with an externally pretrained small model to reject bad components; a final +5 claim still requires Qwen3-1.7B on the locked ten-task suite. No model test was run in preparing this review.

## 5. Closing: independent suggestions and blind spots

### 5A. The missing shared variable is the *location of loss*, not its label

The project describes knowledge loss and optimizer instability as separate categories. The literature suggests they can share a cause: a training regime or output-head representation that **distorts the final decision margin**, even when much of the internal computation remains useful. The mechanism shown by [Linger and Lose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902) is **not** established on Qwen, and the recoverability shown by [BitDistill](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998) is **not** general memory restoration. A *paired margin-and-feature damage map* would connect the two at far lower cost than indiscriminate training. This is a hypothesis motivated by the sources, not a published five-point claim.

### 5B. Investigate *tied-head gradient arbitration* as a training-only control

A tied matrix simultaneously serves as input lookups and output classifiers. OneBit's extra factors improve a different, untied layer representation ([code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit/main/transformers/src/transformers/models/bitnet.py)); BitDistill's inserted normalization changes another architecture ([Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998)); Linger and Lose reduces the learning rate on an **untied** head ([Section 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902)). **My inference:** separate *gradient contributions* from the lookup and output roles during training, arbitrate them by training-only rules, and merge updates into the **same single binary tensor** with no extra inference storage. This avoids copying an untied-head repair into a tied model as though the two settings were equivalent. It is a proposed optimizer control, not a demonstrated result.

### 5C. Couple sign moves to their optimal *group scale* and to a hard flip budget

JointQ shows that group scales and discrete codes must be priced together, but only at 2 to 4 bits and primarily on large models ([Section 3 and Appendix E](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384)). ΣΔ-Sampler shows that an uncontrolled code-update rate can destroy an already-good discrete checkpoint ([Table S5](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621)). A natural **new candidate**, not a published proven algorithm, is **scale-repriced, budgeted binary sign migration**: propose only a limited number of changes per update, reprice the affected group's FP16 scale, and use teacher-relative decision loss plus untouched validation data as acceptance criteria. This acts on the exact allowed degrees of freedom rather than relying on nondeployable low-rank vectors. The possible benefit is training efficiency and recovery quality; the risk is that locally beneficial moves fail to compose globally.

### 5D. Do not mistake a high percentage for a strong absolute model

At 135M, BinaryLLM can lose major HellaSwag and ARC-e scores while preserving a respectable task-average ratio ([Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974)). At the opposite extreme, a task-specific BitDistill student can report more than 100% relative retention on MNLI yet be unrelated to a general reasoning benchmark ([Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998)). The product scoreboard must be locked to one teacher and ten evaluation tasks, but the science scoreboard must retain **per-task errors, confidence, lost/gained decisions, and cost**. Otherwise a five-point step can conceal new failures.

### 5E. Keep research claims within their bit accounting

The most serious systematic blind spot in this paper set is **interface exclusion**. OneBit keeps learned FP16 magnitude vectors and an output norm ([source code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xuyuzhuang11/OneBit/main/transformers/src/transformers/models/bitnet.py)); BinaryLLM leaves embedding/head out of its quoted body bit rate ([Section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974)); ΣΔ-Sampler keeps the LM head continuous ([Section 2.4](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621)); BitDistill is ternary and adds SubLN ([Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998)). Their results are **evidence for repair components**, not prior victories at 1.125-bpw full-model precision. A failed exact-head extension is not proof their methods are wrong; a successful body-only result is not proof our objective is close.

### 5F. Independent ordering

My ranked **research allocation**, not a forecast of additive benchmark gains, is:

1. **Teacher-directed, task-aware distillation under a legal tied binary codec**, because the best relevant *measured task recovery* at 1.7B comes from [BitDistill](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13998). Minimize specialized-data leakage and assess every other benchmark for regression.
2. **Joint scale/sign repricing plus bounded sign-motion on a working binary checkpoint**, because [JointQ](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/pdf/2611.20384) and [ΣΔ-Sampler](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2610.22621) each identify one necessary ingredient but do not demonstrate the combination or exact codec.
3. **Tied-output gradient/precision pathology controls**, because [Linger and Lose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32902) indicates a numerically important failure class that may not be representational memory loss.
4. **Consistency-preserving progressive training**, because [BinaryLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06974) isolates a reproducible smaller benefit at 135M, though the training budget and full-precision interface limit its immediate value.

**Final verdict:** The public literature contains **credible mechanisms capable of moving individual small-model task scores by well over five raw points**, especially task-specific ternary distillation. It does **not yet demonstrate** an independently reproducible five-point increase in the specified ten-task retention at **1.125-bpw strict binary Qwen3-1.7B with a tied binary head**. The next useful contribution is to separate damage at the last decision from damage to internal representation, and to adapt only the ingredients that can exist without extra deployed bits. Nothing in this review awards a product-scoreboard milestone.
