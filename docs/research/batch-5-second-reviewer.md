# 1 Bit Summit: Independent research review, Batch 5

**Research date:** 24 November 2026  
**Scope:** Published low-bit recovery methods, independent Bonsai forensics, and the prerequisites for a defensible Apple Silicon ten-benchmark baseline.  
**Status:** Literature, repository, model-card and evaluation-configuration review only. No model weights were downloaded, no language model was trained, and no model was evaluated. A claim labeled **reported** is a primary author's result, not independent replication. A claim labeled **secondary** is information from an earlier research plan or search excerpt where the underlying document could not be independently reopened.  
**Confidence convention:** Probabilities below are subjective probabilities of delivering **at least +5 percentage points of fixed-suite, FP16-teacher-relative retention** compared with a *matched, own-recipe binary baseline*. They are not probabilities that the underlying paper is correct.

## 1. Framing and findings that change the assignment

The published Bonsai 1.7B comparison provides a ten-task BF16 score of 58.24 and an all-binary score of 40.88: retention 70.20%. At the same teacher and ten-task weighting, a five-percentage-point increase requires `0.05 × 58.24 × 10 = 29.12` additional *summed task score points*, or 2.912 higher points on the ten-task average. **Our own formula does not yet have a measured ten-task baseline.** These are an external reference and a common scoring yardstick, not our earned starting rung. Primary artifact: [PrismML Bonsai demo and whitepaper listing](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo), `1-bit-bonsai-8b-whitepaper.pdf`, Table 9; [released model](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Bonsai-1.7B-gguf).

Three cautions determine the verdicts:

1. **A model's ten-task average is not portable across papers.** ScaleQ reports a Qwen3-1.7B FP16 MATH-500 score of 90.80 and HumanEval+ 76.22, whereas the specified Bonsai evaluation reports 74.0 and 57.3 for its Qwen3-1.7B FP16 teacher. That alone prohibits using ScaleQ's student scores to assert a gain against Bonsai's ten-task baseline without matching prompts, thinking mode, output budget, answer extraction and harness. Sources: [ScaleQ Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1), [Bonsai Table 9](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo).
2. **Ternary 1.58-bit, two-bit and body-only 1-bit are not the project codec.** The legal output is `q_i = s_g b_i`, `b_i ∈ {−1,+1}`, precisely one FP16 scale for each contiguous 128-weight group, with tied input/output embedding included. A learned bias, asymmetric zero-point, a third code `0`, unmerged adapter, output FP16 head, runtime rotation matrix, or extra per-channel coefficients changes the contract unless proved exactly foldable or accounted for. See [Bonsai's Q1_0 specification](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Bonsai-1.7B-gguf).
3. **An already binary checkpoint is not the same optimization state as the original FP16 parent.** CAT-Q, YAQA and LR-QAT normally use pretrained real-valued weights or their grid residuals. A deployed sign/scale checkpoint has lost those latent magnitudes. Reconstructing trainable latent weights from the binary checkpoint changes the optimization path; importing the parent as a teacher or a separate training-state initializer is legitimate, but must be disclosed. Sources: [CAT-Q §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26650v1), [LR-QAT §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06385), [YAQA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tseng26b.html).

## 2. Item 1: Additional low-bit training recipes and repairs

### 2A. The most consequential result is *which calibration text* is used

**ScaleQ-1.58** is a post-training ternarization method, not traditional end-to-end model retraining. It applies CAT-Q's sliding-window calibration with **Attend to Your Own Thoughts (AYOT)**: choose clean mathematics and code prompts from **MetaMathQA and OpenCodeInstruct**, run the *same FP16 teacher to be compressed* to produce its own reasoning traces and final answers, concatenate prompt + trace + answer, then optimize quantization on those contexts. Default calibration budget: **4 million context tokens**, not four million steps or proof of a 4M-token QAT recovery run. The paper claims no overlap of its sampled calibration prompts with the five named evaluation sets. It does not publish a BFCL-v3 or IFBench overlap audit. [ScaleQ §2, §3.1 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1).

The decisive Qwen3-4B fixed-budget comparison from ScaleQ Table 4 is:

| Calibration input, same W1.58 target | MATH-500 | GSM8K | Omni-MATH | HumanEval+ | MBPP+ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Generic WikiText2 | 0.00 | 2.65 | 0.43 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| Generic C4 | 0.00 | 14.48 | 2.71 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| Math/code question-answer pairs | 24.20 | 28.65 | 5.04 | 17.88 | 11.38 |
| Stronger-LLM-generated reasoning traces | 31.80 | 30.78 | 6.98 | 16.46 | 14.29 |
| **The target teacher's own reasoning traces** | **58.40** | **61.56** | **14.93** | **53.98** | **39.15** |

Source: [ScaleQ Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1). The measured effect is larger than most quantizer-parameter ablations because the *distribution of activations presented during reconstruction* changes. This is a ternary PTQ phenomenon at 4B, not evidence of the same numerical boost at 1-bit and 1.7B. The 4B experiment cannot independently isolate “own reasoning” from all possible differences in generated trace quality and trajectory length.

**Critical transfer point:** Instead of designing a general recovery corpus and hoping it covers the failed benchmarks, construct a *task-stratified, contamination-screened* calibration bank where the teacher generates its own trajectories under **thinking off**, matching the ten-task evaluation. No benchmark evaluation item, paraphrase, or closely equivalent problem should enter training. Maintain separate protected test items and training-only distributions. Also keep a broader general-text arm because ScaleQ Table 8 shows task-specific gains can coincide with deterioration in ordinary language perplexity or commonsense accuracy. [ScaleQ Tables 6 to 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1).

**The multi-domain trap:** On Qwen3-4B, ScaleQ Table 7 reports ProofWriter average 39.50 when the calibration corpus covers math/code, but **82.21** after adding ProofWriter training-domain examples, against FP16 **84.92**. In Table 8 adding generic data to math/code improves commonsense average from **53.23 to 57.27** and WikiText/C4 mean perplexity from **35.34 to 28.18**, while the math/code aggregate is similar. Adding scientific-domain data raises ProofWriter average to **83.92**, with shifts elsewhere. [ScaleQ Tables 7 to 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1). These results do *not* prove generalized model knowledge was recovered; they instead illustrate how strongly restoration depends on which behavior was represented in the calibration activations.

**At our exact target size, ScaleQ has an FP16-teacher comparison, but on a different evaluation suite:**

| Qwen3-1.7B on ScaleQ's own evaluation | FP16 teacher | Ternary 4M context tokens | Student/teacher |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MATH-500 | 90.80 | 38.60 | 42.51% |
| GSM8K | 84.08 | 54.35 | 64.64% |
| Omni-MATH | 27.76 | 12.23 | 44.06% |
| HumanEval+ | 76.22 | 34.14 | 44.79% |
| MBPP+ | 53.97 | 33.33 | 61.76% |

Source: [ScaleQ Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1). This directly refutes reading its “90.52%” headline as retention against the Qwen3 FP16 teacher. That headline compares the ternary output against the separate **BitNet 2B4T** model, trained from scratch, rather than the same teacher. The five benchmarks, scoring conditions and likely reasoning settings cannot be substituted for Bonsai's ten-task thinking-off suite.

ScaleQ's token sweep on 4B (Table 2) reports a monotonic MATH-500 progression of **20.9 at 256K**, 38.6 at 512K, 47.6 at 1M, 55.2 at 2M, 58.4 at 4M, 62.31 at 8M and 66.2 at 16M context tokens. The paper explicitly keeps the epoch count and hyperparameters fixed for this comparison. This is compelling evidence for a calibration-token performance curve, not a projection that a similarly shaped binary curve will reach 99%. [ScaleQ Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1).

**Additional action suggested by the evidence:** Define the smallest mixed calibration set that covers all **ten task *types***, not ten sets of benchmark items: math proofs, arithmetic word problems, code synthesis, API selection and abstention, constrained instructions, multihop reasoning and closed-book factual questioning. Measure output-alignment and full-benchmark gains separately. This is a proposed design, not a published experimental result.

### 2B. CAT-Q's actual optimization variables and the constraints of a binary adaptation

**CAT-Q** begins with real-valued pretrained matrices `W` and performs g128 ternary quantization. Its trainable parameters at a group level are modulation factors `δμ`, `δα`, `δΔ`: shifted/scaled proxy weights `W_hat=(W-μ)/α`, `μ=μ0+δμ·α0`, `α=δα·α0`, and threshold `Δ=δΔ·0.5`. Ternary codes use `+1/0/−1` according to whether the modulated weight exceeds the threshold. Critically, `μ` is a *search/selection aid*, **not an extra per-group deployed bias**: their default weight approximation is `W ≈ αT`, without `μ`, and their Table 9 finds this marginally better than retaining `μ`. [CAT-Q §§2.1 to 2.3, Tables 9 to 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26650v1).

Their softened ternarization uses a continuous transition until a fraction `γ=0.8` of calibration training and hard ternarization for the last 20%; sharpness parameter `s0=30`. The reconstruction loss is taken over a *sliding group of layers*, with other parts held fixed or as teacher targets, rather than using the full ten-task accuracy as the optimization objective. The printed CAT-Q calibration settings: **512 C4 sequences × 2,048 tokens = 1,048,576 unique positions**, **60 epochs**, batch size **3**, AdamW, modulation LR **0.001** with linear decay to zero, group size **128**. These are distinct counts: 1M unique calibration positions is **not** the total positions processed across 60 repeated epochs. [CAT-Q §2.4 and Appendix B, Table A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26650v1).

Qwen3-1.7B results (CAT-Q Table 1, five commonsense accuracy scores, different suite):

| Precision | PIQA | ARC-E | ARC-C | HellaSwag | WinoGrande | Mean | Retention |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FP16 | 72.25 | 69.95 | 42.66 | 60.36 | 61.88 | 61.42 | 100.0% |
| CAT-Q W1.58A16 | 68.40 | 55.88 | 28.16 | 47.77 | 54.83 | 51.01 | **83.05%** |
| CAT-Q W1.58A8 | 68.16 | 54.94 | 28.67 | 47.22 | 54.62 | 50.72 | **82.58%** |

Source: [CAT-Q Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26650v1). The student is still ternary, activations are A16 or A8, and this is **not** PrismML's ten-task score.

CAT-Q's detailed **Qwen3-4B** component ablation (Table 5) exposes where the method's performance originates:

| Configuration | Five-task commonsense mean |
|---|---:|
| SliderQuant baseline, no modulation or soft ternarization | 40.16 |
| `δμ` only | 46.10 |
| `δα` only | 49.93 |
| `δμ+δα` | 51.41 |
| `δμ+δα+δΔ` | 53.19 |
| `δμ+δα+ST` | 55.53 |
| All three modulation parameters + ST | **57.06** |

Source: [CAT-Q Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26650v1). With fixed hyperparameters the gamma setting **0.8 -> 57.06** beats hard-only `γ=1.0 -> 55.02`; sharpness **s0=30 -> 57.06**, **s0=1000 -> 50.82**; a learned threshold -> **57.06** versus fixed Δ=0.5 -> **55.53**. Group size 64 yields 57.25, g128 yields 57.06, g256 56.09, g512 55.49, so g128 is viable but *not uniquely optimal* on that five-task ternary metric. Sources: [CAT-Q Tables 6 to 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26650v1).

**Transfer to exact binary:** Modulation of the sign decision boundary and a smooth-to-hard training schedule are compatible *in principle*. The ternary zero threshold is not: binary has no zero code, so `δΔ` needs to become a binary decision-margin/flip policy, not a disguised third symbol or added full-precision residual. A calibrated group scale `α` can be written as FP16. For an already-binary model the missing original `W` must be replaced by a documented latent initialization or by a strictly binary-aware objective; one cannot repeat CAT-Q's `W_hat=(W−μ)/α` on unknown original W and call it an exact reproduction. The published code home [BitTern](https://github.com/IntelChina-AI/BitTern) publicly lists CAT-Q **checkpoints, inference, evaluation**, but I did not verify a complete official end-to-end training script from that root. Alternative community trainer: [penk/ternary-quench](https://github.com/penk/ternary-quench), which credits CAT-Q's quantizer, but independent feature and reproducibility parity were not audited.

### 2C. YAQA: optimize the actual output distribution, not a local reconstruction score

**Model-Preserving Adaptive Rounding (YAQA)** is a *post-training* discrete assignment optimizer. It targets the quadratic approximation to **whole-network output KL** using **Kronecker-factored approximations of the Hessian** for linear layers; it is not a replacement optimizer for billions of pretraining gradient steps. Its reported improvements in output KL over GPTQ/LDLQ are large, but the inspected evidence is concentrated on **2, 3 and 4 bits** with Llama, Gemma and Qwen examples. See [ICML paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tseng26b.html), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22988), [author code](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/yaqa-quantization).

A publicly discussed Qwen3-8B example with QTIP reports 2-bit output KL **0.285 for the baseline versus 0.205 for YAQA-B**; a 4-bit MMLU example actually favors LDLQ at **73.04 versus YAQA-B 72.78**, a useful demonstration that KL improvement and task accuracy need not align. These *specific values* were seen via a third-party summary, not independently verified against the complete primary appendix, and are deliberately not treated as a quantitative projection for 1.7B W1. [Detailed external reading](https://greeksharifa.github.io/en/posts/model-preserving-adaptive-rounding/), [official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tseng26b.html).

**Transfer:** A sign change can be posed as a constrained discrete rounding move, and the correct end objective is full-model KL or benchmark-relevant teacher decisions. However, 1-bit has **only two candidates**, and the group scale couples all 128 moves. A Hessian sketch derived from a 2/4-bit codebook and separate layer groups may require a different solver. An initially binary starting point can still be optimized if its scale and sign variables are explicitly represented, but the original FP16 teacher is needed to define full-model teacher-relative KL. **Evidence for +5 at strict 1-bit: absent.** Cheapest decisive information: exact target codebook support in the released YAQA quantizer and a matched 1-bit run on a fixed budget, then benchmark accuracy rather than KL alone.

### 2D. LR-QAT: low-rank latent search folded into discrete codes

**LR-QAT** freezes a quantization-grid version of the source model (`Φ0`), trains low-rank factors `A,B` **inside the rounding operator**, and trains quantization scale parameters `s`. Deployed weights are obtained by rounding/clipping the updated grid, which incorporates the adapters into the integer weight array. No residual LoRA factor is required at inference. This is conceptually relevant to economical code flipping even though the paper's trained representation is not binary. [LR-QAT method, equations and §§3 to 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06385), [code](https://github.com/qualcomm-ai-research/LR-QAT).

Actual published experimental coverage: LLaMA-1 7B, LLaMA-2 7B/13B, LLaMA-3 8B, Mistral 7B, **not 3B or below**. It quantizes attention and FFN linear layers but keeps embedding, final classification head and RMSNorm frozen/unquantized. Tested **W4/W3/W2**, W2 asymmetric for comparison, per-channel and g128. Text: **SlimPajama**; batch **32**, sequence length **1024**, **10,000 steps** = **327.68M processed positions** if fully populated; ablations 1,000 steps, approximately **32.77M positions**. Baseline selection on 512 Wikipedia validation sequences, final zero-shot LM-eval v0.4.2 and WikiText2. Sources: [LR-QAT §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06385), [Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06385).

Illustrative 2-bit g128 LLaMA-2 7B zero-shot six-task mean: FP16 **70.47**, RTN **41.30**, PEQA **64.65**, LR-QAT **65.27** (all reported in the paper's appendix). LR-QAT's *increment beyond the similar scale-training PEQA baseline* is **0.62 points**, not the 23.97-point distance from naive RTN; comparing only against RTN exaggerates the unique gain of LR-QAT. [LR-QAT Appendix A, Table A5/A6](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06385).

Ablations: rank `r=1` already helps; ranks up to 32 improve somewhat and >32 show no further gain at the published short budget, `r=32` default. Aggressively integer-downcasting the latent grid loses necessary fractional information: at W3, an INT3 base can break badly, whereas a Q3.5 fixed-point grid or LoftQ-like initialization recovers performance. Full-model QAT and LR-QAT are closer in loss than memory footprint. The core transferable idea is **train a low-rank, temporary *search perturbation* that ultimately changes only 128-bit sign groups and their scales**. But original fractional weights and teacher behavior may still be essential; no source showed strict g128 1-bit, tied embedding, Qwen3-1.7B results. [LR-QAT Tables 1 to 3 and Appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06385).

### 2E. JointQ and the OneCompression source tree

**JointQ**, published as [arXiv 2611.20384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20384), couples *integer weight assignments and group scales* in a reconstruction/QPTQ solver, instead of freezing scale before selecting integers. It lives in [FujitsuResearch/OneCompression](https://github.com/FujitsuResearch/OneCompression). The official [vLLM integration guide](https://github.com/FujitsuResearch/OneCompression/blob/main/docs/user-guide/vllm-inference.md) states that JointQ exports **GPTQ-compatible tensor layout** and is served by vLLM's existing GPTQ plugin. This establishes an engineering route, not our binary-format support.

What can be verified from the inspected coverage is **2 to 4-bit PTQ**, with larger model sizes and GPTQ-family baselines, rather than whole-model W1 Qwen3-1.7B trained from a deployed sign/scale checkpoint. JointQ is therefore **a possible scale-code alternating-solver inspiration**, not evidence that binary STE has been beaten at the target. The repo contains other algorithms, including a distinct `OneBit` feature in later releases; do not assume it is JointQ or evidence from JointQ's paper. [OneCompression release notes](https://github.com/FujitsuResearch/OneCompression/releases), [quantizer guide](https://github.com/FujitsuResearch/OneCompression/blob/main/docs/user-guide/configuration.md). Exact JointQ training/calibration tokens, all paper-table numerical comparisons, and strict-binary code paths remain unresolved in this pass because the complete primary paper/implementation could not be inspected.

### 2F. PRISM: factual recall is not a synonym for all multiple-choice accuracy

The Springer 2026 paper [**PRISM: a diagnostic framework for parametric recall degradation in quantized large language models**](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10791-026-10580-5) examines **closed-book factual recall** under controlled quantization of attention versus MLP. Publisher-indexed results report Qwen2.5-3B **1.00 to 0.16** recall after **3-bit MLP-only** quantization, versus **1.00 to 0.86** for attention-only; the disparity also appears on Llama3.2-3B and Gemma2-2B. The paper describes equal-weight-mass, equal measured dequantization error, calibrated GPTQ, seed and annotation controls. These are **publisher-indexed descriptions**, not a full article inspection. [Primary DOI page and indexed abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10791-026-10580-5).

**Transfer:** It is rational to stratify damaged items into direct fact recall versus inference, and to inspect FFN/SwiGLU sublayers at higher priority than attention for *parametric knowledge* loss. It is not rational to declare that all of MMLU-Redux or GPQA Diamond's loss originates in FFNs. Those tasks mix memorized facts, multistep reasoning, prompt parsing and selecting among distractors. The diagnostic paper does not establish a g128 binary *repair*; it identifies a vulnerability. Do not infer the remedy is to leave MLP in FP16, since that breaks the codec.

### 2H. Item 1 verdict, priorities and five-point probability

1. **Highest posterior for useful transfer:** teacher-self-generated calibration trajectories, stratified by mathematics, code, tools, knowledge and instructions, combined with matched general text. Causal evidence: ScaleQ's within-method calibration swaps and large task-domain gains. Missing proof: 1-bit-only, Qwen3-1.7B, thinking off, whole embedding, ten-task held-out scores.
2. **Second:** CAT-Q-inspired sign decision modulation and progressive hardening, *binary-adapted* rather than uncritically importing ternary zero thresholds. Missing: how to initialize after binary deployment and whether scales/signs can move enough.
3. **Third:** LR-QAT temporary low-rank search that **merges into packed signs and scales**, potentially lowering optimizer-state memory. Missing: 1-bit trainability and no hidden extra coefficients.
4. **Fourth:** output-KL-aware sign and scale choices (YAQA/JointQ), with an evaluation check that KL improves the right tasks. Missing: scalable binary solver and exact codebook compliance.

**Subjective probability of +5 points attributable to a coherent Item 1 recipe:** **before 20%, after 33%**. This is an upward revision for evidence that teacher-conditioned **data distribution** materially matters, not for a demonstrated binary success. A much weaker marginal probability, approximately 12%, attaches to taking any single named paper unchanged and achieving +5 on the strict format. Additional compute: calibration/teacher inference, optimization over all g128 scales and sign latents, and ten-task validation. Stored bits and inference operations must remain unchanged. Cheapest resolution: access exact original checkpoint plus a small, matched **binary** calibration comparison, with fixed teacher/student harness and out-of-domain controls; this is a proposed future verification, **not** a test conducted in this review.

### 3B. The key forensic comparison: exactly what was compared

**Second-generation ternary (reported by sky-is-green, secondary summary):** The investigators align PrismML's released ternary 27B weights with its original backbone after applying the published runtime transform. Rounding the transformed original reproduces approximately **92% of the released ternary codes**, rather than approximately **33% chance agreement** among three balanced code symbols. Yet applying direct quantization independently across all **402** ternary tensors yields a catastrophic reported perplexity change **18.6 to 23,606**. Their error-feedback rounded variant with estimated curvature moves code choices *further away* from the released model than ordinary transformed rounding. Source trail: [sky-is-green repository](https://github.com/sky-is-green/bonsai2-ternary-forensics), secondary documentation `lowbit-formula-plan-v21.md`, §2B (S73).

This establishes, conditional on the study's unpublished-in-this-session tensor alignment being correct, that the released ternary codes are *near* a rotated base's codes and that naive independent rounding is not a usable functional reconstruction. **It does not logically establish that 8% of ternary codes were changed by gradient training**. Other algorithms can move codes: calibrated coordinate optimization, beam/greedy discrete search, exact-equivalence transformations omitted in alignment, data-dependent error feedback, checkpoint-version differences, or a mixture with training. Their conclusion that this residue reflects learned movement is a plausible **inference**, not a uniquely identified mechanism.

**First-generation binary is a different target.** The earlier first-generation Bonsai 1.7B has a different public weight fingerprint: approximately **28 to 29%** sign disagreement with Qwen3-1.7B, with near-half of original tiny-magnitude weights changing sign, and group-scale ratios approximately **2.1 to 2.26** versus naive original mean absolute magnitude. The reported 2.257× statistic from [ThakiCloud's audit](https://github.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro) is a **median over per-matrix medians**, not a model-wide weighted median over all 128-weight groups. Only matched two-dimensional body tensors were counted in the referenced audit, so its denominator is **not automatically the fully binary whole-model parameter count**. See [ThakiCloud REPORT.md](https://github.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro/blob/main/REPORT.md) and [PrismML unpacked release](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Bonsai-1.7B-unpacked).

The first generation lacks the second generation's documented runtime Hadamard rotation in the same form. Consequently **a recipe inferred from Bonsai 2 27B cannot be asserted to be the original Bonsai 1.7B recipe**. Reports of large positive scale ratios and sign movements are compatible with a strong function-space error-compensation optimizer *and* with additional QAT. They are not a proof of either. ThakiCloud's preference for a compensation interpretation is an inference; competing forensic interpretations based on magnitude-dependent sign movement remain plausible until checked against known-training references. Independent comparison of first- versus second-generation *ternary* fingerprints, as summarized in v21: first-generation nonzero sign changes around **12.7%**, group-scale ratio around **1.96**, zero-code share around **36 to 41%**; second generation zero share around **32.8%**, often **42 of 128 zero codes per group**, with scales closer to naive in a rotated basis. These are reported diagnostics under potentially different alignment conventions, not a direct binary training trajectory. [Bonsai 2 official paper index](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo/blob/main/bonsai-2-27b-whitepaper.pdf), [sky-is-green](https://github.com/sky-is-green/bonsai2-ternary-forensics).

### 3D. Learning-rate schedule, failed recipes and confounds

From the secondary reading of the [recipe ledger](https://github.com/sky-is-green/bonsai2-ternary-forensics):

- **Rotation inside the forward/training loop** was associated with much better reproduction performance. A one-time rotation for calibration and a trainable rotated forward implementation are not interchangeable; the rotation changes which directions are grouped and optimized and whether the training state is coherent with deployment.
- **Managed learning-rate decay** was reported essential for avoiding a pattern in which validation improves and later diverges. The secondary synopsis does **not** give a verified schedule formula. Therefore we cannot adjudicate the requested comparison between *plateau-triggered decay* (“drop LR when held-out loss stalls”) and *preplanned managed decay* (“decay according to a schedule”). Both can be described loosely as managed; **the crucial distinction is what triggers the decrease** and whether the optimizer reaches a sign-change-stable neighborhood before its rate falls.
- **Teacher distillation** appeared in every successful run per the summary, but an otherwise-identical “distillation OFF” ablation was **not** reported there. Hence “the winning recipes used distillation” is supported at this evidence tier; “distillation was causally necessary” is **not**.
- Reported **higher learning rates, learnable scales, magnitude-dependent gating and re-projection** failed to provide additional gains within that project's settings. Negative findings need source run IDs and matched compute to generalize. A failed scale-learning implementation is not a universal theorem that training scales is ineffective at 1 bit.
- **Mirror-descent maps** reportedly performed poorly (7 to 206× teacher perplexity). This tests particular update maps, hyperparameters and training regimes, not all optimization through mirror maps nor a uniquely authenticated PrismML conversion recipe.
- A claimed near-teacher **small-Shakespeare** performance does not imply benchmark portability, as the same project's reported WikiText results are materially weaker. Training on a small repeated corpus risks prompt memorization, overfitting and mismatch with general tasks. The decisive evidence is disjoint text evaluated under a pinned method.

**Retraction/holdout warning:** The [HF discussions](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-2-27B-gguf/discussions/44) contain indexed debate over whether a favorable perplexity result was evaluated on disjoint held-out contexts. I could not open the complete thread, replies, edit history or gate reports; **I cannot certify the exact claims retracted, which checkpoints remain clean, or that 1.137× used the same holdout policy as the other numbers**. No favorable conclusion above treats a disputed/calibration-adjacent number as a definitive generalization result. The project's failure register and the three gate reports must be reconciled against their final runs, not merely read as historical narrative.

### 3E. Other public forensic routes and what actually transfers to binary 1.7B

1. [ThakiCloud's first-generation Bonsai reproduction](https://github.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro) provides the closer **same-parent, same-precision weight fingerprint** for the target Bonsai 1.7B. It does not uniquely identify whether the released binary checkpoint was updated by gradient training. Its audited signs and scales are more immediately informative for binary start choice than a 27B rotated ternary code-match statistic.
2. [OpenBonsai](https://github.com/esokullu/openbonsai) and [AbdelH2O's ternary code study](https://github.com/AbdelH2O/ternary-bonsai-study) provide independent reconstruction work, though results differ in model, format and metric. A ternary reproduction matching perplexity but not GSM8K or HumanEval is positive evidence **against** using reciprocal perplexity as our primary success criterion.
3. [Official PrismML first- and second-generation papers](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo) establish weights, format, scores and runtime transforms, but do not disclose a complete training loss, dataset and token budget for exact reproduction. The potential Caltech [patent application](https://patents.google.com/) describes a related mirror-descent family; a shared institutional relationship does not prove that it was used to produce any specific released checkpoint.
4. [Community ternary Qwen3-1.7B reproduction](https://huggingface.co/alllwang/bonsai-repro-1.7B-ternary) is a more useful known-training control because it reportedly exposes a checkpoint, source and token budget. It is a **weaker precision match**, but permits direct pairing with the same pretrained Qwen teacher if its claimed lineage and full file manifest check out.

**Starting from the released 1-bit Bonsai model changes the problem.** It contains deployed sign codes and scales, not a complete record of the original FP16 magnitudes or optimizer momentum. A successful ternary procedure that requires full-precision latent positions or rotating *three*-valued code assignments is not directly transplantable. Recovering with a fresh latent sign/scale optimizer is possible in principle, but it must export the exact binary g128 representation and demonstrate that its gain comes from the new update, not simply from extra data or more tokens.

### 3F. Item 2 verdict, posterior and cheapest evidence

**Decision:** Do **not** choose an exact Qwen3-1.7B binary training schedule based solely on the independent Bonsai 2 forensics. The available record provides substantial motivation for in-loop transformation, teacher distillation and controlled LR decay, but cannot select plateau-triggered versus scheduled decay or certify the best clean held-out run. Its unusually good 1MB-corpus result is an optimistic *local* bound, not expected ten-task retention. It remains valuable as a list of mechanistic hypotheses and reproducibility controls.

**Subjective +5-point probability from uniquely Item 2-inspired intervention: before 18%, after 20%.** The evidence mildly strengthens the case for combined rotation/distillation/decay, but incomplete primary run logs and the severe domain sensitivity prevent a large posterior increase. Inference cost may be zero if an exact reparameterization folds into final weights, but an explicit runtime rotation would have to be justified and its storage/inference burden measured; trained bits must remain exactly g128 binary. Training cost is some nonzero additional processed-token and checkpoint-selection budget; no defensible numeric estimate is derivable from the incomplete ledger.

**Cheapest way to resolve:** Obtain the open repo's exact frozen run ledger, last commit and three gate reports through a functioning plain-text or archive route, then **read** the complete first- and last-iteration LR traces for each controlled pair, corpus/heldout hashes and direct score table. No LLM model run is necessary merely to resolve documentary disagreements. If the source cannot be obtained, the honest result is “schedule not established,” not an invented learning-rate prescription. A later *single* controlled run would be a separate empirical question and is not part of this literature-only review.

## 4. Item 3: A defensible first ten-benchmark run on Apple Silicon

### 4A. Which Qwen is the teacher, and how to construct the *same prompt*

The official [Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B) is the **pretrained-and-post-trained** model, not the base-only checkpoint [Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-Base](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-Base). Confusing them changes the teacher and invalidates retention ratios. The official card documents **Apache-2.0**, **1.7B parameters total**, **approximately 1.4B excluding embeddings**, **28 decoder layers**, **16 query heads and 8 KV heads** under GQA, and advertised **32,768-token context**. The exact [configuration file](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B/blob/main/config.json) adds: `model_type=qwen3`; `hidden_size=2048`; `intermediate_size=6144`; `head_dim=128`; `num_hidden_layers=28`; `num_attention_heads=16`; `num_key_value_heads=8`; `vocab_size=151936`; `tie_word_embeddings=true`; `max_position_embeddings=40960`; `rms_norm_eps=1e-6`; `rope_theta=1000000`; and BF16 dtype. **Card's 32,768 and configuration's 40,960 are different published values**. Do not silently change the run context according to whichever is most generous. Pin the effective context supported by the runtime/model and record whether a test item exceeds it.

The model card's decoding guidance is crucial: `tokenizer.apply_chat_template(..., enable_thinking=True)` is **the default**. `enable_thinking=False` is the *hard* switch producing a non-thinking assistant prefix. The `/no_think` text marker is a distinct, softer prompt-control behavior and is not guaranteed to produce identical token IDs. With thinking **on**, Qwen recommends temperature **0.6**, top-p **0.95**, top-k **20** and explicitly warns against greedy sampling due to repetition risks. With thinking **off**, it suggests temperature **0.7**, top-p **0.8**, top-k **20**. The project however requires **PrismML's stated thinking-off, greedy benchmark protocol**, not Qwen's recommendation for casual chat. Both teacher and student must use the same fixed decoding policy even if it is not the author's default. Source: [Qwen mode examples and best practices](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B#switching-between-thinking-and-non-thinking-mode).

Further requirements are not optional details: exact tokenizer revision; chat template Jinja content; BOS/EOS/assistant start; generation prefill; whether questions are wrapped in a system instruction; tool schema rendering; whether a final answer is extracted before/after `<think>` tags; assistant output stopping rules; and use of raw completions versus `/v1/chat/completions`. The model card decodes thinking content using **token ID 151668 as `</think>`**, but no-think output should not require the same splitting. Source: [Qwen official usage example](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B). The safe comparison is to hash the **actual prompt token-ID sequence** produced by each arm on the same task, not just compare the human-readable request.

**Required model identity manifest:** Qwen HF repository and immutable commit revision; `config.json` SHA-256; `tokenizer.json` and `tokenizer_config.json` SHA-256; safetensors index and shard SHA-256; model training stage; and an actual `tie_word_embeddings` check in the loaded graph. The child should record its own HF immutable commit and GGUF contents hash, plus its claimed Qwen parent revision. Without these, two “Qwen3-1.7B” baselines can have different outputs for perfectly ordinary reasons.

The documentation specifies `bfcl-eval==2025.10.27.1`, recommends `is_fc_model=True` for native function-calling models, and exposes `underscore_to_dot` formatting. It reports category families **AST_NON_LIVE, AST_LIVE, RELEVANCE, MULTI_TURN**. All counts from [EvalScope bfcl_v3.md, Data Statistics](https://github.com/modelscope/evalscope/blob/main/docs/en/benchmarks/bfcl_v3.md#data-statistics).

**Two easy ways to produce meaningless BFCL scores:**

1. Evaluate the packaged `train` split as though it were legitimate training data because of its label. In this package it is the published **benchmark evaluation split**, and must be treated as held-out test material. Calibration/training on those 4,441 items or paraphrases risks contamination.
2. Change the aggregation. A simple item-weighted accuracy allows the **1,053 `live_multiple` + 882 `live_irrelevance` = 1,935** examples to carry nearly **43.6%** of the total sample count, whereas BFCL's official category-level macro averaging can give much greater weight to rare categories (for example, `live_parallel` has just **16**). The published Bonsai **34.9** cannot be declared to equal one or the other until the exact PrismML report/configured aggregator is retrieved. Separately score individual subsets, the four broader families and the specific top-level metric used by the paper.

**Server-format danger:** `is_fc_model=True` can send native `tools` JSON structures to a server that may not implement Qwen's tool-call template the same way as PrismML's vLLM reference, whereas `False` changes the task into text-only function calling. Qwen's **tool schema and tool-call formatter**, not only the underlying weights, must be checked on identical serialized prompts. A successful localhost chat-completion ping does **not** demonstrate that BFCL is runnable in the intended native function-call mode. Pin the `bfcl-eval` package revision and the EvalScope integration it wraps. [EvalScope reference](https://github.com/modelscope/evalscope/blob/main/docs/en/benchmarks/bfcl_v3.md).

### 4E. Other benchmark traps: code execution, external judging and nondeterminism

**EvalScope:** The publisher's version, **1.4.2**, must be frozen as an installable package/version rather than copied from the moving `main` branch. Inspect the exact 1.4.2 task adapters, few-shot configuration, chat versus generate path, dataset revision, prompt builder, parser, stop-token policy and grouping. Current [EvalScope release history](https://github.com/modelscope/evalscope/releases) demonstrates that later releases fix BFCL aggregate duplication, MMLU-Redux answer parsing and IFEval/GPQA reproducibility issues. These later fixes may be desirable for a new project-specific metric but **cannot silently be applied while calling the result a reproduction of the older published PrismML run**. It is sensible to maintain two labelled evaluation protocols: publisher-compatible v1.4.2 and a separately versioned modern corrected harness, with no cross-protocol rung claims.

**HumanEval+ and MBPP+:** The “+” variants involve executing model-produced code against expanded hidden/unit tests, rather than merely comparing textual completions. [EvalPlus benchmark/reference implementation](https://github.com/evalplus/evalplus) should be pinned by git SHA. The program-execution environment is a security boundary. Do **not** execute arbitrary model code in the host process that owns model files, secrets or internet access. Use a disposable, locally isolated sandbox/container/VM with no network, read-only benchmark fixtures, unprivileged UID, CPU/RAM/time limits, controlled permitted packages/Python version, test process isolation and preserved compiler/runtime errors. Macs do not gain safety merely by running locally. Use code-only evaluation derived from the benchmark contract, not a general shell with the user's files mounted. `pass@1` is not interchangeable with multi-sample `pass@k`; decoding must be fixed.

**MATH, GPQA and knowledge parsing:** The project plan records PrismML's rule-based extraction **with an LLM judge fallback only for parser failures**, specifically a Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite judge in its source summary. That is a **separate evaluator model**, not the model under test, and it is a score-changing dependency: exact judge prompts and prompts sent to it, retry/fallback criteria, output normalization, rate and version drift, and answer-label handling would have to be frozen. The user forbids credentials and paid service. Under that constraint, **an exact reproduction of a score dependent on an unavailable external Gemini judge cannot be certified**. A local deterministic parser-only benchmark is legitimate but is a **new, clearly named protocol**. Compare all candidate students and the teacher through the *same* alternative protocol and report how often the rule parser could not grade; do not artificially count it as the publisher's 70.2% reproduction. The source of the judge detail is secondary: v21, section describing [PrismML PDF Appendix B](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo/blob/main/1-bit-bonsai-8b-whitepaper.pdf).

**Sampling/seed repeatability:** The v21 plan attributes **2 to 5 absolute IFEval points** variation between nominally identical non-deterministic runs to PrismML's Appendix B. Thus `temperature=0` alone is not proof of deterministic GPU output in all settings. Record random seed, exact decoding algorithm, ordering, batch size, concurrency, backend, KV-cache dtype, repeat run and response path; use both **paired question bootstrap intervals** and **conversion-seed variation** if claiming a small product gain. Because the goal counts five **retention** points (29.12 summed task points under this teacher), a score fluctuation of 2 to 5 *on one benchmark* could matter but cannot alone explain the entire five-point **aggregate retention** rung. [PrismML whitepaper index](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo).

**Resource availability on Apple:** The target's small binary checkpoint fits on Apple-class hardware, but benchmark *memory* is not just model weights: hidden output/KV buffers, long contexts, BFCL tool-schema strings, concurrency, runner scripts and untrusted-code sandboxes also consume resources. For reproducibility, pin Apple chip/model, RAM, macOS release, Metal runtime, thread/GPU-layer counts, `n_ctx`, batch/ubatch, flash attention, KV format and CPU fallback. Running a 1.7B model on a different accelerator backend from PrismML's H100 may change rounding or final answer at tight decision boundaries even when the original weights are byte-identical. Distinguish **artifact parity**, **prompt parity**, **logit sanity parity** and **benchmark protocol parity**.

### 4F. Another nominally same-format Qwen3-1.7B child, and published-score disagreement

A separate public organization, [Precision Numerics](https://precisionnumerics.ai/blog/boole-1p7b), publishes [Boole-1.7B](https://huggingface.co/precision-numerics/Boole-1.7B), which search-indexed source content identifies as a **247 MB, Qwen3-1.7B-derived 1-bit model**. Its publisher reports **ten-task score 50.55** against its **own** Qwen teacher **58.83**, i.e. **85.93%**. The same publisher's comparison lists the **PrismML binary Bonsai 1.7B at 44.97**, rather than PrismML's original published **40.88**, under the publisher's setup. Publisher article: [Introducing Boole-1.7B](https://precisionnumerics.ai/blog/boole-1p7b), [public file listing](https://huggingface.co/precision-numerics/Boole-1.7B/tree/main). Current page fetch of its model files and score methodology **failed**, and the project's training recipe/budget and strict binary tensor coverage were **not independently reconstructed**. Treat its claimed comparability as *publisher assertion*, not as an audited strict-codec fact.

This is nevertheless a **very important non-equivalence observation**. The **same named original Qwen teacher is reported at 58.24 by PrismML but 58.83 by Precision Numerics**, a small disagreement that may arise from harness revision, prompts, parsing, model revision, determinism or task aggregation. More consequentially, **the same named Bonsai binary checkpoint is scored 40.88 by PrismML and 44.97 by Precision Numerics**, a **4.09-point ten-task-average difference**. Relative to PrismML teacher 58.24 that would appear as about **7.02 percentage points of retention**, *larger than our next entire five-point rung*, despite no reported change to the Bonsai weights. **It is not a verified seven-point model improvement.** This mismatch illustrates why a reproduction certificate is a precondition for claiming new science. Do not mix publisher results even if they say “same suite” until the per-task prompts, artifacts, parsers and deterministic settings are reconciled. Source for the alternative within-publisher scores: [Precision Numerics comparative report](https://precisionnumerics.ai/blog/boole-1p7b); original: [Bonsai Table 9](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo/blob/main/1-bit-bonsai-8b-whitepaper.pdf).

The within-PRECISION-NUMERICS comparison **Boole 50.55 versus Bonsai 44.97 = +5.58 average points** is less vulnerable to between-publisher contamination *if* those two models were truly evaluated with the same underlying suite and parsing, but still requires verifying the exact file IDs, job settings and Q1_0-versus-proprietary format. Importantly, its **research training recipe remains unverified**, so it is an outside artifact to benchmark, not a documented way to earn five points. A public model card claiming a format is not a tensor-level proof. If an alternate model uses codebook, auxiliary factors or full-precision head, its 1-bit file-size marketing does not satisfy the contract.

Other nominal low-bit Qwen3-1.7B artifacts include [PrismML Ternary Bonsai 1.7B](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-1.7B-gguf), using **three-valued g128** and a **2.125-bpw GGUF packing** (34 bytes per 128) rather than our binary codec, and generic [Unsloth Qwen3-1.7B GGUF variants](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3-1.7B-GGUF), whose mixed types, embedding exceptions and effective BPW differ by quantization preset. Their results are comparative evidence, **not admissible product rungs** without format and model-coverage verification.

### 4G. Evidence gates and required per-item record for the Apple run

**Gate A, artifact identity:** public release source, revision/hash for each model and tokenizer, exact file bytes and GGUF model/tensor metadata, declared parent, stored tensor types and group size. Verify that the **entire** embedding/LM-head and body satisfy binary coverage and that all scales remain FP16 g128. Any exception must be counted explicitly and its size reported.

**Gate B, runtime identity:** a pinned llama.cpp commit verified to accept Q1_0, Apple hardware and software inventory, identical template support for FP16 and binary, reproducible token IDs and decoding caps; a **fixed-prefix logit/dequantization sanity check** against one reference path. MLX must be separately certified; a generic upstream installation is not sufficient if its 1-bit PR is unmerged.

**Gate C, protocol identity:** exact task datasets and immutable revisions, EvalScope v1.4.2 plus BFCL package/version, every generated prompt/token sequence, zero-shot/few-shot configuration, score aggregation, answer grader, local code sandbox, model output budgets and stop strings. Resolve the **external judge** restriction and freeze either a reproducible local grader or a permitted official grader before making a PrismML-equivalence claim.

**Gate D, score identity:** the **teacher must be run first on the same Apple server and dataset snapshot** as each binary student. Record per-task and **per-item** answers and failures, not just final accuracy. The final project retention is `(sum of the ten student percentage scores)/(sum of the ten teacher percentage scores)`. Teacher variance should be assessed; zero-score tasks and task-specific parser errors must be visible. Preserve benchmark items out of the training/calibration corpus.

**Proposed minimum per-item schema** (not code or a performed experiment): `protocol_id`; `model_artifact_sha256`; `tokenizer_revision`; `runtime_commit`; `dataset_name`, `dataset_revision`, `subset`, `item_id`; `prompt_text_hash`, `prompt_token_ids_hash`; `thinking_flag`; generation settings including `temperature`, `seed`, `top_p`, `top_k`, `max_new_tokens`, `stop_ids`; `raw_response`; extracted answer/tool-call AST; `parser_version`; `judge_version_or_none`; gold label/test outcome; `correct`, `invalid`, `timeout`, `refusal`, `format_error`; grader details; response length; execution errors; and a stable hash of this evaluation record. Keep the full item text inside controlled test-only storage, not in the training dataset.

These gates distinguish four scores frequently conflated in low-bit reports:

1. **Published PrismML H100 result:** table 9, external reference and intended protocol.
2. **Apple reproduction of PrismML H100 protocol:** valid only once equivalent tokenizer/templating, grading and dataset are demonstrated; exact accelerator-level token identity may remain imperfect.
3. **Apple internally controlled comparison:** valid for *our* five-point scoreboards if both teacher and student use one frozen Apple protocol, even if it does not numerically reproduce PrismML's published 58.24/40.88.
4. **Alternative corrected harness score:** valid for robustness/science if named separately, never silently merged with an older score series.

### 4H. Item 3 verdict, posterior and cheapest remaining checks

**Decision:** A trustworthy Apple baseline is achievable in principle with released GGUF Q1_0 and pinned llama.cpp, but a literal duplicate of the publisher's **vLLM 0.15.1 on H100 plus potentially external LLM-judge fallback** is **not yet demonstrably reproducible without changing the hardware/service contract**. The immediate risk is not model quality; it is scoring a different model with a different chat template, tool-call mode or grader and then mistaking a protocol change for retained capability.

**Subjective probability Item 3 itself produces +5 points by changing the model:** **before 0%, after 0%**. Measurement does not alter weights. **Separate probability of obtaining a defensible internally matched ten-task Apple baseline**, with the sources and constraints identified here: **before 50%, after 75%**, judgment only, conditional on resolving BFCL transport and judge availability. Probability of a bit-identical H100 benchmark reproduction is **not established**. Work is predominantly validation/setup and protected benchmark execution, with no extra inference bit budget for the deployed model. The lowest-cost next actions are documentary and software checks: pin exact Hugging Face revisions and GGUF metadata, identify publisher's EvalScope configs and judge policy, test fixed-prefix token IDs and logit sanity, then run the teacher and Bonsai Q1_0 on a controlled local protocol. **None of those tests has been performed as part of this review.**

## 5. Cross-item conclusions, blind spots and independent recommendations

**5A. The major overlooked independent variable is the text the *teacher itself* generates for calibration.** ScaleQ's strongest controlled data contrast is sufficiently large to justify elevating text selection above another unvalidated exotic binary optimizer. The interesting hypothesis is **task-conditioned internal activation repair**, not simply “more math examples.” The next five-point question is whether teacher-produced trajectories about *training-domain* math, code, tool schemas, factual retrieval and instruction constraints can preserve decision boundaries across all ten tasks, rather than specializing to math/code while sacrificing commonsense or tool-call refusal. This is unproven at binary g128 and must be tracked as a cross-task tradeoff. [ScaleQ Table 4 and data ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078v1).

**5B. Measure training effects at two levels, with no metric substitution.** The product scoreboard remains exact teacher-relative ten-task benchmark retention at fixed 1.125 bpw. The science scoreboard should retain student-versus-teacher *individual item disagreement*, capability-family loss, tool-call schema errors, math answer-extraction errors, free-generation outputs, logit decision margins and held-out likelihood. A small Shakespeare or WikiText reciprocal perplexity result is not a “percentage of intelligence.” The same model's published ten-task score also depends on evaluator version enough to require a frozen protocol. [Sky-is-green project](https://github.com/sky-is-green/bonsai2-ternary-forensics), [EvalScope repository](https://github.com/modelscope/evalscope).

**5C. Two different questions about Bonsai must remain distinct.** “Can someone recover a useful Qwen3-1.7B ternary model using rotated-basis distillation?” is supported by public independent reproduction reports, with incomplete audit here. “Was first-generation Bonsai 1.7B made by QAT instead of compensated rounding?” is **not** settled by either a 27B rotated-ternary code match or by 29% binary sign moves. A credible causal answer needs a known-training g128 binary parent-child reference and an apples-to-apples sign/scale audit. The user-access-restricted ParetoQ family cannot be that reference under the no-form rule. [ThakiCloud](https://github.com/ThakiCloud/bonsai-1bit-repro), [sky-is-green](https://github.com/sky-is-green/bonsai2-ternary-forensics), [PrismML released weights](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Bonsai-1.7B-unpacked).

**5D. The benchmark should be an executable contract before it becomes a leaderboard.** Treat the released GGUF binary and the source FP16 as two models operating under one frozen Apple protocol; preserve the original PrismML score separately. A benchmark mismatch as large as the **40.88 versus 44.97** reported Bonsai average can exceed an entire five-point retention milestone without any weight changes. If Apple cannot reproduce the external score because the official judging service or patches are unavailable, publish the discrepancy and establish **one fixed internally valid ruler**, rather than discarding the benchmark or claiming exact publisher parity. [PrismML whitepaper](https://github.com/PrismML-Eng/Bonsai-demo/blob/main/1-bit-bonsai-8b-whitepaper.pdf), [Precision Numerics cross-model evaluation](https://precisionnumerics.ai/blog/boole-1p7b).

**5E. Architecture-neutral mathematical opportunity, not an advertised paper result:** search *function-preserving hidden-neuron permutations* specifically to change which 128-weight columns share an FP16 scale, then do data-conditioned binary sign/scale fitting while preserving the entire model's functional equivalence **before** quantization. For SwiGLU, matching permutations of gate/up rows and down columns are exactly function-preserving. The offline permutation can be folded into weights with no extra deployed parameters or inference instructions. This offers a plausible way to improve initial and subsequent binary fitting without changing the codebook, but is **not published evidence of five benchmark points at 1.7B** and must be separated from ScaleQ/CAT-Q training claims. Sources for nearby rather than identical precedents: [PermuQuant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09503), [function-preserving coordinate permutations](https://huggingface.co/blog/nmicic/int-llm-coordinate-permutation). Do not combine it with six other interventions in the first causal comparison, or the science scoreboard will not know what helped.

**5F. Aggregate probability, not to be added across items:** Given the newly inspected data-selection evidence, incomplete forensics and unresolved benchmark-equivalence details, my subjective probability that a **well-controlled combination of Batch 5 research directions** produces a matched **+5-point improvement over our own future strict-binary Qwen3-1.7B baseline** is **35%**, versus **22% before this batch**. That is a hypothesis-ranking judgment, not an observed score, and it should not be multiplied or summed with the individual item probabilities. The project's own baseline is still unmeasured. No candidate's +5-point product trophy has been earned by literature review alone.

**Remaining documentary blockers:** full sky-is-green source corpus including three gates and actual run traces; complete JointQ/YAQA/PRISM appendix/code audit; precise public ScaleQ calibration-code provenance; the original PrismML EvalScope v1.4.2 launch configs/patches and external-judge policy; and verified source-code/format+file-hash audit of Boole-1.7B. All are explicitly research gaps, not assumed absent from the world.
